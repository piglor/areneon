# Candidate features

The `probe-damage` feature sets, their transport and encoding, the play-time sidecar, the pool and bench.

Part of the architecture documentation split out of `CLAUDE.md` (index: [README](README.md)). Ticket references such as `#1179` point to Redmine (`redmine.piglor.com/projects/areneon`), where measured figures and verdicts are recorded.

**Declared history and the `move_outcome` kind** (ADR-022 erratum E-22.5.3, slice H2, #1340; stacked on the history facts of slice H1, #1339; `mechanics-history.ts`, `mechanics-outcome.ts`, `mechanics-guard.ts`; minor changes within v1 of the three mechanics contracts, generated TS and pydantic types, corpus samples `mechanics-query/{valid,invalid,reader-invalid}/*history*` and `*move-outcome*`, `mechanics-result/*move-outcome*`, `*history-unknown`, `mechanics-config/valid/regmc-move-outcome`). **Every query without the new block or kind keeps its bytes and its answer:** `MECHANICS_ADAPTER_FORMAT` stays 2 (no existing answer changed; pinned), the committed config is untouched (so `mechanics_config_hash` and every identity naming it stay), and the new behaviour is named by `OUTCOME_FORMAT` (1, `mechanics-outcome.ts`), which the `probe-outcome/1` feature set of slice H3 puts in its identity. **History block:** `Combatant.history` (the facts of one Pokemon) and a `history` of `damage`, `speed_order` and `move_outcome` queries (`turn`, `field_last_move`, per side `total_fainted` and `pending_slots`), all optional, closed, integers only. A fact is `{kind: "known", value}` or `{kind: "unknown", ends: [first, second]}` (the profile's kind-tagged union; one wrapper per value type: count, boolean, id or null, attackers, ids, volatiles, slots). The facts are those of H1, each declared through the engine's own field or method on the scenario battle: `move_actions` (`activeMoveActions`), `stall_counter` (the stall condition added through the engine, then its counter set to the declared value, one turn left; 0 is none; `world-state.ts` `writeStallCounter` beside the world builder's `writeProtectCounter`; the condition id is the committed world writers record's `protect_counter` condition), `times_attacked`, `hurt_this_turn` (`hurtThisTurn`), `attacked_by` (the engine's `gotAttacked`, each entry names a declared Pokemon and the damage it dealt), `last_move_failed` (`moveLastTurnResult`, false when failed), `stats_raised_this_turn` / `stats_lowered_this_turn`, `ate_berry`, `last_item`, `moves_used` (`moveSlots[].used`, each id a move of the set), `volatiles` (`addVolatileAt` of the world builder, `starts` times for a stacking volatile such as Stockpile, `moved_before` (default true, as the builder reads an unsaid fact) for the start handlers that read the queue, Taunt's base length being one longer for a target that had already moved, then `remainingTurns` from the declared `turn`: the engine's own length less the turns since it began, a volatile that has run out is a refusal), `total_fainted`, `field_last_move` (`battle.lastMove`) and `pending_slots` (the engine's `addSlotCondition` with `battle.turn` set to the start turn, so the engine's own start handler dates it: Wish, Future Sight). The declared turn is set on the battle. Opponent PP is never declared. A declared fact the engine refuses (an id it lacks, a move not in the set, a volatile run out, an attacker that is not another declared Pokemon) is a `MechanicsProbeError` (the `ProbeRefusal` path of #1246), never a crash; a draw the declaration makes (a volatile with a random length) is `unsupported` `random_effect`. Reader rules the profile cannot say (`historyProblems`, `moveOutcomeProblems`): a volatile or a slot condition needs `history.turn`, an attacker and a slot position name declared Pokemon, `asked` names a move action and `target` a declared Pokemon, and the `speed_order` action rules apply. A declared history is applied after the declared state, then the declared stages and items are written again (a volatile with a stage effect, Stockpile, would otherwise change them). **Two ends rule:** a query with `unknown` facts is asked once with every unknown fact at its first end, once more per unknown fact whose ends differ with that fact alone at its second end, and once with all differing facts at their second end (`historyVariants`): each unknown fact alone plus the all-second-ends corner are covered; other joint interactions are not modelled (all 2^n corners would be too many; a decision where the erratum left room). `probeMechanics` keeps the answer when all variants are equal and answers `unsupported` with the new reason `history_unknown` (a closed reason added to the result contract and to `Unsupported`; `PlainReason` / `plainReason` in `mechanics-result.ts` narrow it away where the feature builders copy a reason: a query with no history cannot be `history_unknown`) when they differ; a refusal of any variant refuses the query. **`move_outcome`** (`mechanics-outcome.ts`): `{kind, sides, actions (the branch's actions, the asked one included), asked, target, state, history?}`; the answer is `{kind: "move_outcome", precondition: passes|chance|fails, precondition_ppm, hit_ppm}`. The scenario is declared as a `speed_order` one (the shared `declare`, `resolveActions` and `evolve` of `mechanics-speed.ts`, now exported; no behaviour change), the history is declared, the Mega Evolutions run, and the engine's own order (`comparePriority` over the queue entries) decides which of the other actions stay queued after the asked one (the actions that act later: `willAct` and `willMove` read them; a switch acts first and is not queued); an action that ties the asked one in the engine's order is `unsupported` `random_effect` (the engine would shuffle). The engine's own move step (`useMoveInner`, entered as the damage probe does, with `activeMoveActions` counted first as `runMove` does) runs with the recording random source answering every `randomChance` with success (`mechanics-random.ts`) until its hit step, where the probe ends it: the accuracy step is run per target and the draws of the asked target are its hit chance (1 with no draw; 0 for a target the accuracy step refuses), a move of a side or the field has no accuracy step and its first `moveHit` is the hit step (hit chance 1), the precondition chance is the product of the draws made before the hit step (the stall draw of Protect: 1/3, 1/9) and `passes` when there is none; a move that never reaches its hit step (a failed `onTry`, no action after Protect, or an immunity step that removes the only target) is `fails` with both chances 0. A target removed by the immunity step while another target remains does NOT make the result `fails`: the accuracy step is reached, so the result is `passes` (or `chance` from the draws before the hit step) with `hit_ppm` 0; H3 must not read `fails` as "the user's own precondition failed". A `chance` is at least 1 ppm (a floor of 0 is clamped to 1, so 0 stays reserved for `fails`). Chances are exact: `floor(10^6 * prod(n) / prod(d))` in `BigInt`. Any draw that is not a `randomChance` before the hit step (a random power, `sample`) is `unsupported` `random_effect`. The probe holds no move, ability, item or condition id. A chance draw answered "success" is a favourable outcome for the draws the probe reads; a draw whose success is a failure (full paralysis, confusion) belongs to the turn-start events that `useMoveInner` does not run. The kind is in scope only when the config lists it: the committed config does not (a `move_outcome` query answers `unsupported` `query_kind` under it), `withMoveOutcome(config)` adds it (another `mechanics_config_hash`, empty `gap_ids`; the Mega row is added when a Mega Evolution is involved, and `checkMechanicsConfig` requires that row in the ledger for the kind too), and the config schema accepts the kind. `declaredOutcomeScenario` is the test helper that returns the declared battle. **Tests:** `test/mechanics-outcome.test.ts` (hand-worked against the pinned engine: the second and third Protect use 333,333 and 111,111 from the `stall` counter, Protect with no action left after it, First Impression after the first action, Belch before and after a berry with its 90 percent accuracy, Last Resort with unused moves and then used ones, Spit Up with no and with two Stockpile layers, Sucker Punch against a damaging branch, a status branch and a switch, a 70 percent move, an immune target, Wide Guard (a move of a side: no accuracy step), a self move aimed with a foe target, the two ends rule with equal and with different answers, every refusal, a tie, a random power, and the declared fields equal the engine's: counter, hits, attackers, volatile age, slot start turn), `test/mechanics-damage-history.test.ts` (Last Respects, Rage Fist, Assurance, Stomping Tantrum and Lash Out scaled by the declared fact, equal bytes on both damage paths, a history that declares nothing or a fact the move does not read keeps the vectors, a speed order keeps its order), `test/mechanics-history-live.test.ts` (the required live test: every committed random-play, Baton Pass, Fling and revival battle replayed on the pinned engine with a spy; at each request of both seats the seat's history facts are declared in a scenario built from the live Pokemon's sets and the scenario's engine fields equal the live battle's; volatiles are declared by the hand-worked tests because they carry random lengths, and an `attackedBy` entry whose attacker has left play is not declared nor compared), `test/mechanics-history.test.ts` (engine-free: resolution, the ends, the reader rules), `test/mechanics-guard.test.ts` (engine-free: the shared random-source guards), `test/mechanics-outcome-unit.test.ts` (engine-free: the exact chance arithmetic, the hit chance of the asked target, how the move step ends, `plainReason`), the corpus tests (`mechanics-query.test.ts`, `samples.test.ts`), and the existing mechanics, feature-builder and identity tests pass unchanged (the `/1` and `/2` `feature_set_hash`es and every existing corpus sample digest are pinned). Not here: the feature set `probe-outcome/1` and its reviewed record (H3), the `outcomes` array on the wire (H4), the encoder (H5).

**History facts** (ADR-022 erratum E-22.5.2, slice H1, #1339; `packages/public-state/src/history-facts.ts`, `ml/areneon_ml/reducer/history.py`; no consumer yet: the probe's `move_outcome` (H2) and the `history_facts` encoder option (H5) read them). The battle history the pinned engine reads from fields of its own and that neither the shared reader nor the world facts track, derived from ONE player's own channel by a module of its own: `HistoryFactsReader` (`feed` a line, `facts()`), `historyFacts`, `historyFactsAtRequests`, input the branded `ownHistoryChannel` (type test: no team, omniscient log, hidden verdict, record or seed; a `|split|` marker is refused). Its own `HISTORY_FACTS_FORMAT` (`history-facts/1`) and file hash (`historyFactsSha256()`, part of the future `probe-outcome/1` identity); it imports nothing and `interpreter.cjs`, `readerSha256()`, `world-facts.ts` and `WORLD_FACTS_FORMAT` are untouched, so no reader, baseline, belief, feature-set or search identity moved. Facts per Pokemon in a position (`active[letter]`, the engine's own keying; the facts of a Pokemon reset with its stint): `move_actions` (`activeMoveActions`), `stall_counter` (`volatiles.stall.counter`, null without the volatile: it follows consecutive successful stalling uses, lasts the turn after a use until that turn's residual, a Baton Pass hands it over per the copy table, a `-activate` of a protect-breaking move deletes it), `times_attacked` (one per move `-damage` with no `[from]` on another Pokemon; a hit a Substitute takes is no number damage in this engine, so it counts none, which the parity test found against the erratum's draft; a delayed attack that lands counts for the target, attributed to the user's position or `attackers_this_turn: null` when the user left play; Transform copies the target's count), `hurt_this_turn` (`hurtThisTurn !== null`: a `-damage` through `Battle.damage`, not a user's own direct cost or Struggle's `[from] recoil`; confusion's hit is `damage()`), `attackers_this_turn`, `last_move_failed` (`moveLastTurnResult === false`, **null when the lines cannot settle it**: only a `cant` (a `BeforeMove` handler that returns null prints a reason in `null_cant_reasons`, recharge, and does not count as failed), an action whose lines are only `-miss`/`-immune`, a damaging hit or a self-targeted move's own effect are settled; a called or reflected move, `-fail`, a zero boost, protect blocks and multi-step moves are null, about a fifth of the compared points), `stats_raised_this_turn` / `stats_lowered_this_turn`, `moves_used` (own Pokemon only, from the own requests' moves with a PP count and the own move lines, by position), `volatile_started`; per roster Pokemon (never reset on a switch): `ate_berry`, `last_item`; per side `total_fainted` and `pending_slot` (Wish and Future Sight with their start turn and the residuals left); on the field `field_last_move` (`battle.lastMove`, the latest `|move|` line). Resets follow the engine (switch-out `clearVolatile` incl. the champions mod's `timesAttacked`; turn rollover, where `hurtThisTurn` and the stat flags are not reset at turn 1 and `attackedBy` is; `ateBerry` never). An Illusion's `|replace|` makes the Pokemon at that position and both its roster entries `unknown` (null) until it leaves play; a line it cannot place is a typed `HistoryFactsError` (`bad_player`, `secret_marker`, `malformed_line`, `unattributable_line`, `stall_unresolved`; code and line index, no text), sticky from that line on. **No rule in code:** `HistoryFactsData` (`packages/showdown-bridge/src/history-facts-data.ts`, `readHistoryFactsData` / `goldenHistoryFactsData`) is read from the pinned engine's own sources: the stall moves and the stall condition's counter constants (`start`, `factor`, `max`), the `[from]` texts of the move lines the engine runs as actions of their own (Round, Dancer) and those run with `externalMove` (no PP), the tags of eaten berries (`eat`, `weaken`), the berries a thrown item feeds, the `-enditem` sources that set `lastItem` (Fling), the items cleared without `useItem` (the balloon), the `-item` sources that restore (Recycle, Harvest) or take (Pickup) a `lastItem`, the `cant` reasons that return null, the moves that break protection and the timed slot conditions with the residuals each lasts (Wish 2, Future Sight 3: the exporter checks the source still has the shapes it reads and refuses otherwise), plus the committed stall-moves and volatile-copy files; the `[from]` tags of a thrown berry's eating (`stealeat`) and of a consumed gem (`gem`) are exported data too (`steal_eat_tags`, `gem_tags`); every extractor that is non-empty at this pin fails closed (a `HistoryFactsDataError`) when its source pattern stops matching, with doctored-source tests; only protocol wire tokens (`[eat]`, `[from]`, `-singleturn`, `Baton Pass`, `Shed Tail`, and the accepted wire words `stall` (the engine's condition id of the copy table), `recoil`, `confusion`, `slot`) are in the module, and the source scan holds EVERY string literal to an explicit accepted list, so a new game-id literal fails until a reviewer adds it. The `OwnChannel` brand is compile-time only: a raw object passed to the TS reader bypasses the secret-marker refusal (the Python reducer refuses a `|split|` itself); the pinned-values test in `history-facts-data.test.ts` also guards the data itself at a pin bump. `HistoryTracker.feed` keeps only the module's own typed refusals (`{refused: code}`); any other exception is a defect and propagates. The Python mirror is a port, not a second design: `HistoryTracker` (`history.py`) takes the same lines and the same data (the hashed file `ml/areneon_ml/reducer/data/history-facts.<format>.json`, `scripts/export-history-facts-data.ts` after a pin bump, `history_facts_data_hash`, merged with `Vocabulary`'s stall moves and copy table into `Vocabulary.history_data`, None for a pin the file was not exported for) and `BattleState.to_dict()` gains the top-level key `history` with the SAME JSON shape as the TS `HistoryFacts` (position-keyed like the engine's fields, not on `PokemonState.recent`, because the facts belong to a stint in a position and the Illusion rule is per position); `STATE_SCHEMA_VERSION` 3, every `reduced/*.json` golden rewritten (the `history` key and the version only, nothing removed), `packages/public-state/test/parity.test.ts` strips `history` like `recent`. Tests: `history-facts-parity.test.ts` of the bridge replays every committed battle (random-play, `unavailable/`, Illusion, Baton Pass and its volatiles, Fling, revival, side-guard, the 200-battle targeted fidelity corpus; the Transform battle has no seeds, so its channels are only read) on the pinned engine with a test-only spy on the live battle and requires at every request of both seats every settled fact equal to the engine's field for both sides' Pokemon (8,535 requests; `ARENEON_HISTORY_PARITY_MAX` and `ARENEON_HISTORY_PARITY_SKIP=random,unavailable,special,fidelity,golden` limit a local run; roster facts are not compared while an unrevealed Illusion is up on the side, since the shown name is not the holder's; the negative control shifts one fact by one); `packages/public-state/test/history-facts-parity.test.ts` holds the TS facts equal to the Python reducer's at 9,474 request points of 500 channels (a Python child, `history_points.py`; needs the root `.venv`, or `ARENEON_PYTHON`); `history-facts.test.ts` and `tests/ml/reducer/test_history.py` (hand-written lines for every fact, reset and refusal), `history-facts-data.test.ts` (the data against the engine source, the file byte for byte, doctored sources). Known limits: `pending_slot` covers the timed slot conditions only (Healing Wish, Lunar Dance and Revival Blessing end on a switch-in and are not tracked); the facts the module cannot settle are null, never guessed, and the two-ends rule of E-22.5.3 turns them into `unsupported` rows; Natural Gift sets `lastItem` with no line (not in the format); a `-activate` that breaks a protection is read for the moves the data names; the targeted fidelity corpus reaches Harvest, Pickup and Feint zero times (the module's data covers them, the parity does not): a corpus extension is a follow-up, which regenerates the fidelity report.

**Candidate feature builder** (ADR-022 tier A, slice D1 builder part, #1026; `packages/showdown-bridge/src/candidate-features.ts`, `damage-aware-view.ts`). `candidateFeatures(engine, configs, channel, team, candidates, memo?)` is a pure function of the player's own `PlayerChannel` (the channel must end in the request being answered, read from its last `|request|` line), the player's own team as brought, the candidate strings and the configs `feature_set_hash` names. It feeds the channel to the shared reader, builds each opposing active's assumed set exactly as the v1 baseline does (the shared `damage-aware-view.ts`: `buildAssumedSet`, the public-status ability rule, `assumedHp`; the belief-based sets are ADR-025 slice 5, #1135, and would be a new feature set id) and asks the probe ONE `damage` query per distinct row `(slot, move position, target position, Mega flag)` that some candidate (pending ones included) uses with a damaging move into an opposing active; a spread or random-target move gets one row per foe it can hit, each a single-defender query; a status move, a switch, a pass and an ally or self target have no row. A request with no move to score (Team Preview, forced switch, wait) has no rows. Rows are returned in canonical order (slot, move, target, Mega false first), so bytes depend neither on candidate order nor on the memo. A row is `computed` with `mean_ppm` (the mean over the 16 no-crit rolls of `min(roll, hp) / hp`, floored, the baseline's `rollsValue` with no KO weight), `max_ppm`, `min_ppm` (the largest and smallest roll, clamped at `hp`, as a share of `hp`) and `ohko_rolls` (the probe's own `ko.ohko_no_crit` count of the 16 rolls, or null when the probe's `ko` is `unsupported`; ADR-016 slice 5 exists, so the row has it from the first feature set), or `unsupported` with the probe's closed reason, never a guess. `hp` is the shown percentage applied to the assumed maximum HP with the record's `hp_rounding`. `featureSetIdentity` / `featureSetHash`: SHA-256 of the JCS of `{feature_set: "probe-damage/1", mechanics_config_hash, assumed_set_config_hash, adapter_format, reader_sha256, version_manifest}`; a change of any part (or of what a row holds) is a new feature set. The per-game memo is `createFeatureMemo()` (`results` keyed by the probe query's hash, the defender's HP stays in the query so it stays in the key; `counts` has `queries`, `probe_runs` and `unsupported_queries`, where `queries` includes the maximum-HP and status-ability queries the assumed sets need). A request that cannot be featured throws `FeatureBuildError` with a code (`no_request`, `reader_flagged` (a line the reader could not take since the previous request), `reader_gap`, `assumed_set`, `probe_refused`) and a fixed message; the caller (D2's `policyActor`) decides what then, and a defect (a shape the baseline does not know) propagates as `DamageAwareError`. The type rule: the channel parameter is the branded `PlayerChannel`, so an `OmniscientLog`, `HiddenVerdicts`, `BattleRecord`, a seed or an unchecked log is a compile error (a type test), and a source scan requires no runner, omniscient or seed import. The shared module `damage-aware-view.ts` holds what the baseline and the builder both read a request with (own combatants, foes, declared field, the memoised probe call, `DamageAwareError`); `damage-aware-actor.ts` uses it and is unchanged in behaviour (its tests pass unmodified; `foesHit` takes the foes as a thunk so a move that hits no foe builds none and asks the probe nothing, as before). Measured cost and memo hit rate: see #1026. Invariance (#1029, `test/candidate-features-invariance.test.ts`, real probe): seeded battle pairs whose p2 teams differ in nature, Stat Points, items, moves 2 to 4 and abilities give byte-identical `CandidateFeatures` (rows and `feature_set_hash`) at every p1 request with an equal channel (at least 20 points over at least 2 pairs); a revealed ability is used as revealed; a builder given the real opponent team through `FeatureConfigs.assume` (a test seam) gives different rows (negative control); a canary scan of never-public names in the JCS bytes; a type test. Not here: latency and the worker-thread pool, the `PolicyQuery` / `TrainingExample` field, the encoder (`probe-damage/2`, incoming threats and turn order, is the next paragraph).

**Feature builder `probe-damage/2`** (ADR-022 erratum E-22.3, #1172; `packages/showdown-bridge/src/candidate-features-2.ts`; the contract fields are #1174 (next paragraph), the invariance extension #1173, the encoder #1175, none of them here). `candidateFeatures2(engine, configs, channel, team, candidates, memo?, trace?)` builds `probe-damage/1`'s `rows` with the very code `candidateFeatures` runs (`damageRows`, exported from `candidate-features.ts`, which `/1` calls unchanged: the two sets give equal `rows` bytes, a test) and four more arrays, all on ADR-018's v1 assumed opponent sets and the shared reader: relations and values under the assumed foe (revealed item, ability, stages, status and Mega forme as revealed; everything else from `damage-aware.v1.json`), never facts about the real opponent, and the docs and labels say so. `feature_set_hash` is `/1`'s preimage with the id `probe-damage/2` (`featureSetIdentity(engine, configs, FEATURE_SET_2)`, which refuses a mechanics config that does not list `speed_order`). **Own Pokemon** (position = index into the request's `side.pokemon`): the living actives of a move request (a fainted one has no row), plus every bench Pokemon some `switch N` candidate names (a forced switch has only these); an active slot where a candidate Mega Evolves also has the own-Mega variant (the Pokemon declared Mega Evolved). A bench Pokemon is read from the request and own team alone (`damageView.ownBench`: stages 0, Mega as the reader's roster shows it, so a Pokemon never in play is no `reader_gap`). **`threats`** (own, mega, foe, move): one single-defender `damage` query per foe revealed move (`AssumedSet.revealed_moves`: damaging, target type reaching an opposing Pokemon by `foeTargetKind`, so single, spread and random targets; a foe with no revealed damaging move has none) into each own Pokemon at its exact HP from its own request; the row values are `/1`'s (`damageValues`: `mean_ppm`, `max_ppm`, `min_ppm`, `ohko_rolls`, or the probe's reason). **`speeds`** (own, mega, foe): exactly one row per own position, own Mega flag and living foe; `relation` `own_first` / `tie` / `foe_first` from the tie groups of one `speed_order` query per group of at most two own positions (a Mega variant alone, declared Mega Evolved), every combatant declared with a switch action so the engine orders them by action speed alone (Trick Room, Tailwind, paralysis, stages, a held Scarf, a Mega's new Speed); a query the probe does not answer is asked again for each own Pokemon alone. **`own_priorities`** (slot, move position, Mega flag) and **`foe_priorities`** (foe, move id): the engine's `priority` key of each own move part of the candidates (status moves, pending candidates and the Mega variant included) and each revealed foe move, from batched `speed_order` queries (round k asks the k-th move of every combatant; the Mega variant asks its Pokemon alone with the action Mega Evolving); a batch that is not `computed` is re-asked one action at a time, so only the offending row is `unsupported` and no row depends on how the moves were batched (a test). The probe resolves each action against a fixed foe target, so a target-dependent priority event is not modelled (as ADR-018). `unsupported` reasons are the probe's closed ones plus `unqueueable` and `fractional_priority`, which `damageView.queryLimited` now names (the memo stores `{limit}` where it stored `null`; `ask` still returns `null` for the baseline, whose behaviour and counts are unchanged). **Decision where E-22.3 is silent:** a pseudo-move the request names that the engine's data lacks (`recharge`, the forced turn after a recharge move) cannot be queued: its `own_priorities` row is `unsupported` with `query_kind` and nobody is asked about it (without this, 15 of 794 sampled decisions were refused as `probe_refused`); the reader rule "every own move part is covered" still holds. Sorted keys: threats by (own, mega false first, foe, move id), speeds by (own, mega, foe), own priorities by (slot, move, mega), foe priorities by (foe, move id); ids compare as code units. Requests: Team Preview and wait give empty arrays; a forced switch gives `threats` and `speeds` for the Pokemon its candidates name and nothing else. The five `FeatureBuildError` codes are unchanged (`refusing` maps the baseline's signals; a speed-order or damage query the engine refuses for a state the builder declared makes its rows `unsupported` with `query_kind` since #1246; `probe_refused` now applies only to the assumed set's own `stats` query). **Known limit, found by the bench:** `mechanics-speed.ts` `declare` sets the terrain before the combatants' statuses, so a burn or other status seen before a Misty Terrain began is refused by the engine ("the engine refused the declared status") in a speed-order query while the damage queries (which declare in another order) accept it: 4 of 794 sampled decisions (0.5%) were refused for that reason on `/2` (none on `/1`); since #1246 such a request is featured with `unsupported` (`query_kind`) rows instead (#1243 later fixed the declare order in the probe). **Feature builder pool, bench and step-2 trigger measurement** (ADR-022 section 4 mitigation 3 and section 5, #1030; `packages/showdown-bridge/src/feature-service.ts`, `feature-pool.ts`, `feature-bench.ts`, `scripts/feature-bench.ts`). `createFeatureService(spec)` is `candidateFeatures` behind a task interface (`FeatureTask`: game key, player, the own channel lines up to the request, own team, candidates, optional `final`): it loads the pinned engine and the reviewed records once, keeps one probe memo per game (dropped after a `final` task) and returns the outcome (`features`, or the `FeatureBuildError` code), the build time in ms and the probe counts the task added. `FeaturePool(size, spec)` runs one service per Node worker thread (a `data:` worker module that imports `feature-service.ts` and only wires messages to `answerMessage`, as the private shard decode does, so no worker script sits outside the coverage gate); `run(tasks)` returns the results in task order whatever order the threads finish in, so the rows are `candidateFeatures`' bytes and never depend on scheduling or size (size 0 runs on the calling thread; a test requires sizes 0, 1 and 3 and a second batch to give identical rows). A game goes to the thread that saw it first (least loaded), so its memo serves its later decisions; a defect in a thread is a `FeaturePoolError`, never a retry. No play or stream-writing process calls the pool yet: it has no consumer until the contract field lands. The bench (`node packages/showdown-bridge/scripts/feature-bench.ts --run-dir R --generation G [--episodes N] [--progress-dir P --progress-games N] [--pools 4,8] [--out FILE]`) is read-only on run directories and prints counts and milliseconds only (no species, move, team or line), with the machine model, logical CPUs, load average and the engine pin: per-decision single-thread latency (median, mean, p95, p99, max) over the move requests of an evenly spaced sample of the generation's Episodes (candidates by `enumerateCandidates` on the request, as `policyActor` makes them; Team Preview and forced switches have no rows and are counted apart), rows per decision, memo hit rate on the probe queries, the `unsupported` rows by reason and errors by code, then the same decisions through each pool size (wall time, decisions per second, rows equal to the single-thread run's). With `--progress-dir` the stored progress games are replayed through the runner from seed, teams and recorded choices (`progressDecisions`; the omniscient log is never read, each seat's channel is the replay's). The generation estimate (`estimateWallTime`) adds the builder's cost twice (at play and again at stream writing; sample mean per seat decision times the control's `counts.decisions`) divided over T threads to the control generation's play plus load from its run-metrics record and compares it with 1.5 times that; it assumes otherwise idle threads and no overlap with play, so it is an estimate, not a run. The probe-dominated rate (EXP-DF2's secondary measure, "computed on assumed sets", `addDominated`): per slot of a recorded choice that plays a move with a computed row, the chosen move's `mean_ppm` (the chosen target's row; any foe's for a spread move; the chosen Mega flag) against the largest computed `mean_ppm` of any row of the same slot; dominated when twice the chosen is below the best; switches, passes, status moves and unsupported rows are counted apart, never in the rate. The bench reads synthetic Episodes and progress games only (it refuses another data class); `max_turns` for the replay comes from the run config; the pool warms every thread before timing and reports the measured speed-up beside the ideal estimate. A caller that omits `final` keeps a game's memo for the pool's lifetime. Tests: `test/feature-pool.test.ts`, `test/feature-bench.test.ts`.
