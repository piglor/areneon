# Team Preview solver and best-response

The Team Preview solver, the hypothetical Team Preview battle, the payoff table and the best-response measurement.

Part of the architecture documentation split out of `CLAUDE.md` (index: [README](README.md)). Ticket references such as `#1179` point to Redmine (`redmine.piglor.com/projects/areneon`), where measured figures and verdicts are recorded.

**Team Preview solver** (ADR-029 §3a, #1253; `packages/opponent-model/src/preview-solver.ts`, `ml/areneon_ml/preview/solver.py`; pure, engine-free, float-free, no consumer yet). `solve(matrix, iterations = 4096, kind)` takes an integer payoff matrix in ppm (0 to 10^6, rows ours, columns the opponent's; constant-sum, the opponent's payoff is 10^6 minus the entry) and returns our mixture, the opponent's mixture (both ppm summing to 10^6) and the exact matrix exploitability (our best pure response against the opponent's average minus the opponent's best against ours, in ppm squared; the Python `Solution.exploitability_ppm()` rounds up). Unconditional regret matching with both sides updated simultaneously; each iterate normalises the positive cumulative regrets by largest remainder (floors, then one ppm per remainder in descending order, ties by lower index; uniform by the same rule when none is positive), the output normalises the integer sum of the iterates the same way. Regrets are exact integers in ppm cubed (BigInt in TS, `int` in Python, so nothing overflows); `regret-matching-plus` floors the cumulative regrets at 0; `exact-support` is not built. Malformed input (kind, iterations outside 1 to 10^6, not rectangular, a dimension outside 1 to 4096, an entry that is not an integer in 0..10^6) is a `SolverError` with a fixed message. `SOLVER_FORMAT` is the string `preview-solver/1` (as `BELIEF_FORMAT` is a string; ADR-029's text says an integer, so the identity ticket #1259 uses the string or the ADR gets an erratum). `MAX_ITERATIONS` (10^6) and `MAX_DIMENSION` (4,096) are this module's own sanity bounds, not ADR values. Golden vectors are computed first in Python (`tests/ml/preview/make_vectors.py` rewrites `fixtures/preview-solver/vectors.json`; a Python test requires the file fresh) and the TS test requires byte-identical outputs on every one, plus falling exploitability over 16, 256 and 4,096 iterations, every committed curve within the regret-matching bound (Hart and Mas-Colell 2000: summed exploitability of the averages at most 2·D·√max(n,m)/√T, D = 10^6 ppm; checked exactly in integers as E²·T ≤ 4·10^24·max(n,m) on both sides), and the 30,000 ppm bound at 4,096 iterations on the random matrices, which is an empirical regression guard over the committed vectors, not a theorem.

**Hypothetical Team Preview battle** (ADR-029 sections 3 and 7, findings N5 and N6, slice 1, #1255; `packages/showdown-bridge/src/hypothetical-preview.ts`). `startHypothetical({engine, format_id, seat, own_team, world_team, stream, world, data_class, snapshot?})` builds ONE in-process battle of the TARGET format on the pinned engine with our six as brought on our seat and a `sampleTeam` world's six on the other (both validated first, on copies, as `runBattle` does; the caller maps the world's `WorldSet`s to engine sets, the bridge does not import the opponent model), engine seed `hypotheticalEngineSeed(stream, world)` = `sodium,` + the first 32 hex of SHA-256 over the JCS of `{purpose: "preview-solve-engine/1", stream: <hex>, world: <index>}` (a Python-checkable preimage; `world` is the world's index as in ADR-026's `search-engine/1`), started once to Team Preview and serialised once. It returns `{manifest, seat, foe, own_channel, foe_channel, foe_request, play, observation}` (`foe_request` and a turn's `request` are opaque `HypotheticalRequest`s with `player`, `phase` and `request_hash` only; `hypotheticalCandidates` gives opaque `HypotheticalCandidate`s `{order, pending}` with no `choice` field, so neither is a `DecodedRequest` or `Candidate`; type tests with `@ts-expect-error`): the channels are the engine's own output split by its `|split|` rule (`playerView`; the opponent seat sees exactly what the format shows a player: the six preview lines, and no sheets: an in-process battle never issues the server's `>show-openteamsheets`, and the pinned snapshot has no open-sheet format, so open-sheet equality is neither supported nor tested; a future open-sheet format needs ADR-027's sheet reveal added to this module's start), and `play(own_choice, foe_choice)` runs a clone (`cloneWorld` + `playChoices` of `search-turn.ts`, the already reviewed `fromJSON` site: this module adds no entry to `FROM_JSON_ALLOWED` or `PRNG_ASSIGN_ALLOWED`) to our seat's next request, returning `{kind: "request", channel, request}` (our hypothetical channel to the first move request, by the engine's split rule: our secret halves only), `{kind: "ended", outcome}` or `{kind: "refused", reason}`; a request that is not a move request (a wait or forced switch) is returned as it is for the caller to class. **Brands:** `HypotheticalChannel` and `HypotheticalObservation` are opaque interfaces (a unique-symbol tag, the seat, the observation kind) that hold NO `lines`, `schema_version`, `identity` or `PlayerChannel`/`Observation` brand; the text lives in module-private WeakMaps, so `playerChannel`, `recordedChannel`, `continueChannel`, `buildObservation`, an Episode writer, the training loader and a real `PolicyQuery` cannot take one (type tests with `@ts-expect-error`; `playerChannel(brand)` also throws at run time). The only way to a worker is `evaluateHypothetical(client, battle, observation, policy_id)`, which, for a client that states a candidate feature set, requires the rows the battle's feature configs made (#1316, below) and sends the EXISTING `evaluate` query (`sampling: {kind: "evaluate"}`, the observation the module built and the request's candidates): the wire contract is unchanged (the worker cannot tell the origin; the guarantee is on the sending side, as ADR-029 section 3 words it, "the worker reaches them only through the solver's own evaluate call"). `PreviewSolveInput` is `{format_id, seat, team, channel, stream}` and has no field for a running battle, `BattleRecord`, `OmniscientLog`, hidden verdicts, battle seed or opponent team (key-set type test). `HYPOTHETICAL_BATTLE_FORMAT` (1) is part of the future solver identity; bump it when the battle is started, cut or observed differently for the same input. `Engine.sim.Battle`'s constructor type gained the optional `send`. Tests (`test/hypothetical-preview.test.ts`): the hypothetical opponent's Team Preview observation and `request_hash` equal a real `runBattle`'s for that seat (same teams, the same engine seed), and our hypothetical first-move-request observation equals the real one for the same orders; the canary (nickname markers in a world's sets appear in the hypothetical opponent's view; the real game, Episodes, `loadTrainingSet` stream and observation are made AFTER the marked solve on the same engine instance and hold none, and the record's team hashes bind the real opponent; the negative controls push hypothetical lines through the unbranded `playerChannel(unknown)` route and into a real record's channel, and the scan flags both); invariance from both seats (a world differing only in natures and Stat Points gives our side an identical hypothetical Team Preview observation AND first-move-request observation after `play()`, while the opponent's own differs; another set of species changes ours); the engine-seed vectors are literals computed with Python hashlib; the observation sent is a frozen copy; stand-in engine cases for no Team Preview, an immediate end and a missing request. The payoff-table builder is the next paragraph; not here: the solver call, the actor and the policy id.

**Team Preview payoff table** (ADR-029 section 3, findings B1, N3 and the N7 clamp, Figure 1, #1256; `packages/showdown-bridge/src/preview-payoff.ts`; a library, no actor, no policy id, no solver call). `buildPayoffTable({config, worlds, client, battle, policy_id, stream})` returns one decision's table `U(s, L)`: rows are OUR strategies, columns the opponent's ordered lead pairs, entries integer ppm. `worlds` are `{index, preview}` with `preview` a started `HypotheticalPreview` (#1255; the caller maps each `sampleTeam` draw to engine sets and starts it with our six, and passes our REAL Team Preview request as `own_request`: `startHypothetical` then takes each of our sets' gender from its `details` (the engine would draw a missing gender from the world's seed and give our side another request in another world; a request that does not match our team is a `HypotheticalError`), and `buildPayoffTable` takes the same `own_request` and refuses (`PayoffError`) any world whose own Team Preview `request_hash` is not the real one, so our strategies come from a request every world agrees on); the module sees no channel text and reaches the worker only through `evaluateHypothetical`. `PayoffConfig` has no defaults (S0-P freezes them): `k_own`, `k_opp`, `own_rule` (`prior-top-k` or `distinct-leads`) and `leaf` (`{kind: "first-request"}` or `{kind: "rollout", turns}`). **Strategies (N3):** `strategiesOf(candidates, prior)` groups the `team ...` orders (360 of four from six) by (ordered lead pair = the first two positions, unordered back set = the rest, sorted): 180 strategies in canonical order of first appearance, probability the sum of the group's priors, played as the group's likelier order (ties the canonically first); `selectOwn` takes our top `k_own` by probability (ties canonical), under `distinct-leads` skipping a strategy whose ordered lead pair was taken. Our strategies come from our Preview network on our own Team Preview observation (world 0's; the same decision in every world). **Opponent columns (B1):** our Preview network evaluated on each world's hypothetical opponent observation, the prior summed by ordered lead pair over every strategy of the world, summed over worlds, top `k_opp` by the sum (ties canonical; `opp[].mean_ppm` is the floor mean). **Opponent back pair:** first-request leaf, the world's most probable back set given L (no draw); rollout leaf, drawn per world from the world's conditional given L, weighted by the strategies' probabilities, by `backDraw` (an integer below the total from the first 8 bytes of SHA-256 over the JCS of `{leads, purpose: "preview-opp-back/1", stream, world}`, vectors computed with Python hashlib); either way the opponent plays that strategy's likelier order. **Entries:** for every (s, L, world) `HypotheticalPreview.play(s.order, opponent order)` to our first move request, the hypothetical observation, the worker's `evaluate`, `value_ppm` CLAMPED to 0..10^6 (`clamped`, `clamped_low`, `clamped_high`); a game that ends first scores by its result (`outcomeScore`, `game_end_entries`); an engine refusal drops the WHOLE world for every cell (ADR-029 section 3: `engine_refusals`, `worlds_dropped`, so all cells average the same worlds), as does a request that is not a move request (`not_move_request`); counts of work done (queries, clamps, attempts) include a dropped world's, `entries` only kept worlds'. `U(s, L)` is the floor mean over the worlds that produced the entry (`contributors` says how many), null where none did; `complete` and `matrix` (the integer solver's input, null when any entry is missing) follow, and `missing_entries` counts the nulls. **Rollout leaf:** the new `HypotheticalPreview.rollout(own, foe, turns, choose)` plays on from the first requests, both seats answered by `choose` (the builder's argmax of the pair's prior over the non-pending candidates, first highest on a tie, through `evaluateHypothetical` on each seat's hypothetical observation, counted in `rollout_choices`) for `turns` move requests of our seat, forced switches answered too and not counted, a waiting seat not asked, then stops at our next move request (at most `max_steps`, default `MAX_ROLLOUT_STEPS` 64, requests answered; a rollout that reaches the cap is NOT a refusal: it returns the request it reached with `capped: true`, in any phase, and the builder values it with the same clamped `evaluate`, counted in `rollout_capped`; `max_rollout_steps` of `PayoffInput` is the test seam); `playChoices` (`search-turn.ts`) gained the opt-in `withFoe` argument that also returns the opponent seat's own lines and request (absent, the result is unchanged). The value at the rollout's end is the same clamped `evaluate`. Counts are integers (`PayoffCounts`: worlds asked and used, Preview queries, entry attempts, entries, value queries, rollout choices, back draws, clamps, game ends, engine refusals, non-move requests, missing entries); `PAYOFF_LEAF_FORMAT` (1) is part of the future solver identity, bump it when an entry is computed differently for the same input. **A pair that reads candidate features (#1316):** `HypotheticalStartInput.features {configs}` (the run's `FeatureConfigs`, `pairFeatureSetup(...).configs`) makes every hypothetical observation carry the rows `buildFeatures` makes (the one dispatch `policyActor` uses) on THAT seat's own hypothetical channel (read as a `PlayerChannel` inside the module only, never leaving it) and own sets, through ADR-018's assumed sets of the shared reader: our rows never read the world's sets (the opponent seat's rows, built only for the rollout's chooser, are its own view and its own sets, which are the world's); one memo per seat per hypothetical battle. `evaluateHypothetical` then sends them as `candidate_features` of the existing `evaluate` query (Team Preview: an empty list), and refuses (`features_pair`) an observation whose rows do not match what the worker stated, rows for a worker that states none, and a worker that states a set when the battle was started without the configs. A request the builder cannot feature (`FeatureBuildError`) is a `HypotheticalFeatureError` (a `HypotheticalError` with the builder's `code`, no content): the payoff table drops that world, counted, never a rule fallback. Absent `features`, every byte is as before. Tests (`test/preview-payoff.test.ts`): 360 orders dedupe to 180 on a six-Pokemon preview, the table on two fixed worlds equals a table worked out in the test from the teams alone (stand-in worker whose Preview prior is a fixed weight per candidate index and whose value is a function of the leads' nicknames and species, so rows, columns, floor means and clamps are computed without the module), the cap, whole-world drops and the real-request rules have their own tests; the first-request observation is identical for three back pairs with the same leads while a rollout with both seats taking the last candidate differs, determinism incl. the rollout leaf and its draws, every count and refusal, the draw vectors; `policy-worker.python.test.ts` (the real worker, skipped without the Python environment) builds first-request and rollout tables twice and requires bit-identical bytes. **Features pair (#1316):** worlds started with the pair's feature configs make every observation carry rows (see the hypothetical battle above); an entry whose request the builder cannot feature (`HypotheticalFeatureError`) drops its whole world like an engine refusal, counted in `counts.worlds_dropped` and, by the builder's code, in `PayoffTable.feature_refusals` (a plain object beside `counts`, not in `PayoffCounts`, so no record contract and no existing byte changed; empty for a pair without features). `PAYOFF_LEAF_FORMAT` is unchanged: no value is computed differently for an existing input.

**Best-response gain, blind control and room (E0-P)** (ADR-029 section 2, ADR-017 E-17.1, ADR-008 E-8.1, #1257; `packages/showdown-bridge/src/best-response.ts`, `best-response-record.ts`, `best-response-cli.ts`, `packages/team-dataset/scripts/best-response.ts`, `ml/areneon_ml/progress/best_response.py`; evaluation-only). `python -m areneon_ml.loop best-response --run-dir R --generation G --mode argmax|sample --pairs N --worlds W --k-own K --own-rule prior-top-k|distinct-leads --leaf first-request|rollout:T --out D --engine-data E [--preview-budget-ms MS] [--eval-dataset-dir DIR --eval-dataset-hash H] [--evaluation-key KEY] [--looks FILE]` verifies the committed generation as the progress evaluation does (a pair with private ancestry is refused), runs the Node script once (no game processes: a game is a function of its index, but the responder's tables make one game cost minutes, so the cost is the experiment's to size), reads the `BestResponseRecord` back with its own reader, ties it to the request, derives the pair-level intervals (`stats.pair_interval`, never stored), appends a `best-response` look (the match `best-response-<mode>`, with the evaluation key when given) to the validation look log and only then renames the directory into place (never overwritten). **The arms,** on every validation pair (`progressPlan`, `progress-seed/1`; `--eval-dataset-*` and `--evaluation-key` as for `progress`) played twice, team A on p1 for an even game and p2 for an odd one: the `mirror` (the evaluated pair on both teams), the `responder` (the responder on team B against the pair on team A), the `blind` control (the same responder against the uniform model) and the `room` arm (the responder on team A against the pair on team B). Outcomes are seen from the focal side (team B's for the first three, team A's for room); a forfeit is a scored loss for its side. BRG is the mean over pairs of the responder's pair score minus the mirror's, `BRG_blind` the same with the blind arm, the exploitation margin their paired difference (responder minus blind) and R the room arm's mean pair score minus one half, each with the two-sided 95% pair-level percentile bootstrap interval of ADR-017 E2 (the E0 go rule's one-sided bounds are #1258's). **The responder** (`decidePreview`) is the future solver's front half without the solver: at its Team Preview request it reads only its own channel and team, computes ADR-025's belief over the other team from the shared reader's opponent half (the composition root's `createOpponentTeams`, generator-matched prior), draws `W` full teams (`sampleTeam`, never the true sets; each normalised by the validator as pool teams are, since the worker's reader checks request names against the team), starts a hypothetical battle per world (`startHypothetical`, the real request as `own_request`) and builds ONE payoff table (`buildPayoffTable`, no game outcome) from the evaluated pair's own networks: rows its top `k_own` strategies, columns EVERY ordered lead pair of the other side (`ALL_COLUMNS`, so the blind control can weigh them equally). It plays the row with the highest expected entry (integer column weights, ties by the strategy's canonical rank) under the model of the evaluated side's play: `argmax` weighs a lead pair by the number of worlds in which the evaluated Preview network's top order leads with it (the new optional `PayoffConfig.argmax_columns` adds `opp[].argmax_worlds`; absent keeps every table's shape), `sample` by the network's mean mass summed by lead pair (`mean_ppm`), `blind` equally. The responder and the blind control of one game share one table (one decision per game and side; both are recorded). Battle play of every seat is the pair's own Battle network in the same mode (`PolicySeat.make` wraps `policyActor` with a `preview_actor`), so only the Preview differs; the games of one pair share the pair's seed and the sampling draws, so two arms that choose the same strategy play the same game. **Fallbacks** (counted per decision in the record: `fallback` with a code): a belief refusal (`species_not_in_prior`, ...), no startable world (`no_world`: every draw refused or a team the validator refuses as a whole; `worlds_refused` and `worlds_unstarted` are counted in a table decision), or no column with weight and an entry in every row (`no_complete_column`) make the seat play the evaluated pair's own Preview (the mirror's choice); a failure of the responder or of the worker inside a decision, or any harness incident, fails the run and nothing is written. **Decisions where the ADR left room:** (1) the solver-mixture model of the evaluated side is not built (the solver seat is #1259), only `argmax` and `sample`; (2) with the rollout leaf the blind control draws the other side's back set from the network's conditional (not exactly uniform over the 180 strategies), so `BRG_blind` is uniform over lead pairs only; with the first-request leaf the back set cannot matter; (3) the argmax model's opponent order given its lead pair is the table's likelier back set, not necessarily the argmax order's own; (4) the table's Preview latency budget is a flag (`plan.responder_preview_budget_ms`, default one hour) applied to every game of every arm, because the table is built inside the decision; (5) a pair that reads candidate features is played (#1316): `evaluated.features` (the run's feature configs from `pairFeatureSetup`, which the command now builds with the belief factories; the worker client states the set) goes to every seat of the evaluated pair and to the hypothetical battles (`ResponderDeps.features`), a world whose request cannot be featured is dropped and counted (`feature_refusals`, `worlds_dropped`), configs without a stating worker, a stating worker without configs and configs that build another set are `features_pair` errors, and the Python command derives the evaluated policy id with `features=features_hash(config)`; (6) no sharding over game processes. **Validity** (Python summary and result, `valid` and `invalid_reasons`; the record itself stays a record of what happened): a result is not valid when the share of decisions that fell back to the pair's own Preview exceeds 50,000 ppm on the responder arm or the room arm (`MAX_FALLBACK_SHARE_PPM`, threshold declared on #1258), when any arm forfeited more than 2% of its games (`MAX_FORFEIT_SHARE_PPM`, E0-P's stopping rule) or when any arm forfeited a game on latency. The payoff table is built inside the responder's timed Preview decision, so the latency budget (an hour by default, the same for every arm) could in principle fall on the responder arms alone: a latency forfeit therefore always invalidates the result rather than scoring as a loss silently. The record holds every per-pair outcome, so #1258 can compute one-sided 5% bounds offline with no rerun. The `validateTeam` call on a sampled team is for its in-place normalisation, not its verdict. **Record** (`packages/schema/contracts/v1/best-response-record.schema.json`, generated TS and pydantic types, samples in `fixtures/contracts/v1/best-response-record/`, integers only, closed): the evaluated pair (generation, manifest hash, checkpoints, mode, policy id), teams (the run's dataset hash, `evaluation_dataset_hash` only for another dataset), the plan (the progress plan fields, `evaluation_key`, the responder's settings and format constants and the belief's identity, prior and config hashes), one tally per arm and one row per game (the four outcomes and both sides' decisions with the table's counts and the chosen strategy, its rank, key and expected value). Both readers (`readBestResponseRecord`, `read_best_response_record`) recount every tally from the rows and check the counts of each table. Labels everywhere: "evaluation-only responder with white-box access to the evaluated policy's weights; a conditional lower bound on validation teams; not the Training gate; never held-out evidence". **Isolation:** `test/best-response-isolation.test.ts` scans every shipped source: only the command's own files name the responder, which imports no Episode, training-loader or stream writer, and a type test shows a responder result is not an Episode or training input. Tests: `best-response.test.ts` (choice rule, weights and a fixed-strategy-versus-uniform arithmetic case), `packages/team-dataset/test/best-response.test.ts` (real engine, real belief, a stand-in worker with a ground truth: team B wins exactly when its first lead is the counter of team A's, so a policy that always plays one fixed strategy is countered by the responder and not by the blind control (a positive margin) while a uniform one gives the two the same weights, the same choice, the same games and a margin of exactly zero; fallbacks, failures, refusals, byte-identical rerun), `best-response-cli.test.ts`, `best-response.python.test.ts` (the real worker and the Python command, skipped without the Python environment) and `tests/ml/progress/test_best_response.py`.

**Team Preview solver as a policy seat** (ADR-029 sections 3, 4, 5 and 7, E-10.1, E-10.2, E-26.1, E-8.1, #1259; `packages/showdown-bridge/src/preview-solve.ts`, `preview-solve-config.ts`, `preview-worlds.ts`, `packages/team-dataset/src/preview-solver-port.ts`; evaluation only, wired into no run, progress evaluation or record yet: that is #1260). **Contract:** `PreviewSolveConfig` (`packages/schema/contracts/v1/preview-solve-config.schema.json`, generated TS and pydantic types, samples in `fixtures/contracts/v1/preview-solve-config/`; integers only, closed): `algorithm {kind: "preview-solve-1"}`, `k_own`, `k_opp`, `selection_rule` (`prior-top-k` | `distinct-leads`), `worlds {n, min_valid, prior {kind, label}}`, `belief_identity_hash`, `leaf` (`value-at-first-request` | `rollout {turns}`, a `kind`-tagged `oneOf` through `$defs`), `solver {kind: regret-matching | regret-matching-plus | exact-support, iterations?}`, `budget_ms` (the per-decision guard; NOTHING in the solve enforces it: the caller's `guardActor` Team Preview budget must be at least it, and `previewSolveBudgetMs(setup)` / `actor.budget_ms` expose it so the evaluation wiring, #1260, sets the Preview budget from the config; it stays in the identity), `a2_clock_floor_ms`, `seed_derivation` (const `preview-solve/1`). Reader rules in TS (`readPreviewSolveConfig`) and Python (`areneon_ml.loop.records.read_preview_solve_config`): `min_valid <= n`; `iterations` present exactly for the iterative kinds. `exact-support` is in the contract (the ADR lists it) but the setup refuses it (`solver_unbuilt`). The committed record `config/preview/<format>/preview-solve-1.v1.json` holds ADR-029's defaults (k 4 and 4, `distinct-leads`, 4 worlds with `min_valid` 3 (the ADR leaves it open), first-request leaf, 4,096 iterations); S0-P freezes them, and a change is a new file. **Identity:** `previewSolveIdentityHash` = SHA-256 of the JCS of `{preview_solve_config_hash, belief_identity_hash, reader_sha256, version_manifest, solver_format, leaf_format, hypothetical_battle_format}`; `solver_format` is the string `SOLVER_FORMAT` (`preview-solver/1`, as the ADR's "integer constants" is wrong for it), `leaf_format` is `PAYOFF_LEAF_FORMAT` (the payoff table's leaf, not the search's `LEAF_FORMAT`), `hypothetical_battle_format` is `HYPOTHETICAL_BATTLE_FORMAT`; the reader refuses a belief identity the config does not name. **Policy id:** `policyId(pair, mode, play, features?, search?, previewSolve?)` and `records.policy_id(..., search=None, preview_solve=None)` add `preview_solve: <hash>` to the ADR-010 preimage only when present, independent of `search` (the solver alone, search alone and both are three ids); every earlier id is unchanged (`fixtures/policy-ids/existing.json` is checked on both sides) and the new vectors (config hash, identity hash, two ids, the solve stream and `u`) were computed with Python hashlib and are pinned in `tests/ml/loop/test_preview_solve_identity.py` and `packages/showdown-bridge/test/preview-solve-config.test.ts`. **Actor:** `previewSolveActor({setup, client, battle, player, policy_id, identity_hash, seed, game, channel_class, base, bank_ms?, on_solve?})` is an A1 `Actor` for ONE seat of ONE game that wraps `base` (the pair's `policyActor`, or ADR-026's search actor; build it with the SOLVER policy id): every request that is not Team Preview goes to `base`. At Team Preview `solvePreview(deps, input)` (input is `PreviewSolveInput`: the format, the seat, the own team, the own channel to the request and the harness stream, nothing else; a type test) reads the reader's opponent half, asks the belief for the opponent's team (`createOpponentTeams`, `sampleTeam`), draws `worlds.n` full teams, starts a hypothetical battle each (`startPreviewWorlds`, shared with the best-response evaluation: its world start and channel reading moved out of `best-response.ts` into `preview-worlds.ts` and `decidePreview` now calls them, behaviour unchanged; the isolation scan applies the forbidden-import and banned-name checks to `preview-solve.ts`, `preview-worlds.ts` and `preview-solve-config.ts`), builds the payoff table (`buildPayoffTable`) from the pair's networks, solves the matrix through the `PreviewSolverPort` (`{format, solve}`; the opponent model's integer regret matching, joined by `createPreviewSolvePorts`; the bridge does not import the opponent model) and plays the strategy drawn from our average mixture: `u` is the first 53 bits of SHA-256 over the JCS of `{seed, game, player, policy_id, request_seq, purpose: "preview-solve/1"}` (`previewSolveU`), the choice the first strategy whose cumulative ppm exceeds `floor(u * 10^6 / 2^53)` (`drawFromMixture`), played as its more probable order. The world stream (worlds, engine seeds, back draws) has its own purpose `preview-solve-worlds/1` (`previewSolveStream`), apart from the draw's, so `u` and the worlds are independent. **Fallbacks** (the wrapped actor plays its own Team Preview, counted by reason in `report().counts.fallbacks`): `belief_refusal` (the belief's code in `belief_refusals`; also every draw refused), `too_few_worlds` (fewer than `min_valid` worlds started, or with every entry produced), `worker_failure` (only a `PolicyReplyError`: an invalid reply to a hypothetical query), `engine_failure` (worlds lost to the engine's own refusal to play on, counted by the table as `engine_refusals`, leaving fewer than `min_valid`); defects are NOT fallbacks and throw as incidents: any `PayoffError` (another own Team Preview request, a prior that does not match its candidates, no worlds), any `HypotheticalError` (our own request or team does not match the hypothetical battle, a format with no Team Preview; only `InvalidTeamError`, a sampled team the validator refuses as a whole, is counted as an unstarted world), an `UnsupportedCandidateError` from a hypothetical query, and a mixture that does not sum to 10^6 (`bad_mixture`); the payoff counts of a solve are kept whether it solved, fell back or threw, `clock_floor` (`bank_ms()` below `a2_clock_floor_ms`, `searchSkipReason`'s rule); the belief's run-setup defects (`BELIEF_DEFECTS`, from a refusal or a draw) throw `PreviewSolveError` `belief_defect`, and any other failure (a dead worker, a protocol error) propagates as an incident. A2 is not wired (no policy is wired into A2, the A2 session records nothing for the solver): the clock rule exists for the day it is, `bank_ms` is the one clock-dependent input and A1 never sets it. Counts per game are integers (`decisions`, `previews`, `solved`, fallbacks by reason, worlds asked / refused / unstarted, the payoff builder's counts summed, and the sums of the solve's exploitability in ppm (rounded up) and support size, to be divided by `solved`); wall time is never stored. **Training:** a solved game has the solver's policy id, which is not the frozen pair's, so `loadTrainingSet` refuses its seats (N12; tested: the Episode of a solved game and its training load equal those of a replay of its choices, and the load under the pair's own id throws `TrainingLoaderError`); `preview-solve-isolation.test.ts` scans that no training, Episode or loader module reaches the solver and that it imports none. **Privacy tests** (`packages/team-dataset/test/preview-solve.test.ts`, real engine, belief, validator and solver, a stand-in worker whose Preview prior reads the observation's own team and whose value hashes the hypothetical channel): opponents that show the same Team Preview and hold other hidden sets and another four give equal tables, mixtures, choices and counts; a port whose worlds are the opponent's real sets (the negative control) gives tables that move with the hidden sets.
