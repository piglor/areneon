# Reducer, encoder, networks and checkpoints

The log-to-state reducer, recent history, the encoder and its options, the networks, checkpoints and the C1/C6 provenance checker.

Part of the architecture documentation split out of `CLAUDE.md` (index: [README](README.md)). Ticket references such as `#1179` point to Redmine (`redmine.piglor.com/projects/areneon`), where measured figures and verdicts are recorded.

**Log-to-state reducer** (`ml/areneon_ml/reducer/`, ADR-010 §3, R10, #838), Python and clean-room (grammar from the repo fixtures, `sim/SIM-PROTOCOL.md` and ADR-006/008; no Showdown client parser): `reduce_channel(player, lines, team, vocab)` turns ONE player's own channel plus that player's own team into a `BattleState` (`own` / `opponent` halves: HP as the player sees it, status, boosts, volatiles, revealed moves/items/abilities, Mega, active slots, weather, terrain, field and side conditions, turn, winner; own exact values come from the player's own requests). Ids are checked against a `Vocabulary` built from the hashed engine-data export, never against lists of its own. It fails closed with typed `ReducerError`s (`UnknownLineError`, `UnknownFieldError`, `MalformedLineError`, `VocabularyError`, `OpponentSecretError`): an unknown line type, tag, positional field or request key raises, a `|split|`, an opponent HP not out of 100 or a request for the other player is an `OpponentSecretError`. A `[from] item:`/`ability:` counts only without `[of]`, and a move is revealed only when chosen (not when called by another effect). Limits, known and deliberate: volatile ids (`-start`/`-end`) are kept as opaque public strings after a shape check, because the engine-data export has no volatile or condition table (a volatile/condition vocabulary, engine-data schema_version 2, is a follow-up the encoder will need); a field or side condition must still be a move or an ability of the vocabulary. Illusion (#851): every switch-in is bound to the Pokemon it is shown as, with a copy of that roster entry as it was before (`_Disguise`), and `|replace|POKEMON|DETAILS` (the engine sends only the new id and details, no HP) says who it was: `_settle` carries everything seen at that position since the switch-in (hit points, status, boosts, volatiles, item and ability changes, moves used) to the real Pokemon's entry, puts the Pokemon it was shown as back as it was before the switch-in (out of play, if the Illusion user took its place), names the real one from the line and records its ability (Illusion). Before that line nothing of the real Pokemon is known: its roster entry stays as the preview gave it (the species is public from `|poke|`; its name, position, hit points, moves and ability are not). The player's own Illusion user is found at its next own request instead (the request lists the real Pokemon in each position, and an own mismatch whose real Pokemon holds Illusion is settled there, so boosts and volatiles are right before the replace; the position keeps the disguise's name for the lines that follow). Limits, deliberate: a disguise that leaves play without a `|replace|` stays attributed to the Pokemon it was shown as (the player saw that Pokemon; a later replace corrects only the stint it ends); a Pokemon that fainted shown as another (no replace) leaves a fainted `phantom` entry (an extra roster entry flagged `phantom` in `to_dict`, not counted against the encoder's `max_team`) when the Pokemon it was shown as is shown again, because a fainted Pokemon cannot come back; the same Pokemon shown in two positions (one is an Illusion user) gets a phantom entry for the second until a replace or an own request says which, and the encoder takes it as the extra row the player sees. Ambiguity the channel cannot settle raises `IllusionError` (a Pokemon shown in three positions, a replace that fits two roster entries or none, a replace with no disguise on record, an own pair the request leaves open). `moves_revealed` is what a Pokemon can have at one time (#938): Transform (`-transform`) replaces the whole list with what the Pokemon uses as the copy (the engine's `moveSlots` is the target's; the target's own list is not copied) and the list it had comes back on switching out or fainting (`moves_base`, not in `to_dict`), and the target's stat stages are copied (the engine's `transformInto`; no line states them), so a Pokemon shows at most four slots plus Struggle (which is no slot); Mimic and Sketch are not moves of the format (not in the engine-data vocabulary: a `|move|` of them is a `VocabularyError`), so their slot replacement is not tracked, and a pin or format that adds them needs it (`-start|P|Mimic|MOVE`, `-activate|P|move: Sketch|MOVE`). `fixtures/showdown/<format>/transform/battle.json` is the captured game that stopped run public-main-002 (a Ditto transformed twice showed nine moves under max_moves 8), `tests/ml/reducer/test_moves.py`. Trace (or Role Play) records the copied ability on both the tracer and the `[of]` target (the tracer's original ability is not tracked). `-activate` of Skill Swap clears both abilities (unknown), as the per-Pokemon result is not stated. Channel lines of the A2 server path (`|t:|`, `|c|`, `|inactive|`) raise here and belong to the A2 adapter slice, which strips them first. The HP colour class the champions mod appends at exactly 50% and 20% (`50/100g`, `20/100y`) is accepted and dropped. Transform (#938): on `-transform` the reducer stashes the user's revealed move list (`moves_base`, not in `to_dict`, so never in an observation) and starts the copy's list empty, filling it only from `|move|` lines; switching out or fainting restores the stash, and the target's stat stages are copied (the engine's `transformInto` copies boosts; `-transform` is SOURCE then TARGET). So a list is at most four slots plus Struggle. The reducer's state logic is not part of the encoder version (`ENCODER_FORMAT` is not bumped), so a reducer change alters the encoded inputs of the games it affects (here those with Transform) without a new encoder version; the bit-identical rerun claim holds per reducer commit, and a run resumed after a reducer change re-verifies only its newest generation. Baton Pass (#1099): the engine's `copyVolatileFrom` gives the incoming Pokemon the outgoing one's stat stages and prints no line for them; the switch line carries `[from] Baton Pass` (Shed Tail passes none), and the reducer and the replay interpreter hand the stages over on such a switch (a reset there made a later `|-boost|`, whose amount is the engine's capped delta, exceed 6: `boost 7 is outside -6..6` stopped run public-main-009). `fixtures/showdown/<format>/baton-pass/battle.json` (`scripts/capture-baton-pass-battle.ts`), `tests/ml/reducer/test_baton_pass.py`. Passed volatiles (#1102): the same `copyVolatileFrom` copies every volatile except those whose condition data says `noCopy` (a Shed Tail switch copies Substitute alone), again with no line. The rule is read from the pinned engine by `packages/showdown-bridge/src/volatile-copy.ts` (`exportVolatileCopy`: the ids of the conditions of the format's mod, from conditions, moves, abilities and items, with `noCopy`, plus the Shed Tail literal, which the export checks the engine source still says) into the reviewed, hashed data file `ml/areneon_ml/reducer/data/volatile-copy.<format>.json` (`volatile_copy_hash` = SHA-256 of the JCS of the other fields; `readVolatileCopy` / `read_volatile_copy` refuse an unknown key, an unsorted list or a wrong hash; `test/volatile-copy.test.ts` re-exports and compares byte for byte, `scripts/export-volatile-copy.ts` rewrites it after a pin bump). The file is package data of `areneon-ml`: `Vocabulary.from_engine_data` loads it by format and keeps it only if its `showdown_commit` is the export's (`not_copied` / `shed_tail_copies`; none for another pin, and then a Baton Pass or Shed Tail switch raises `VocabularyError`, never a guess). The reducer and the interpreter keep on such a switch exactly the outgoing slot's volatiles the rule passes (the stat stages for Baton Pass only, and `ability_suppressed` while `gastroacid` passes: Gastro Acid prints only `-endability`), then the outgoing Pokemon keeps none. The interpreter takes the rule as `dex.volatile_copy` (the replay page embeds it; the `@areneon/public-state` wrapper adds the one committed table to a dex that has none; the raw `.cjs` without it flags the switch line rather than guess). A volatile the engine prints under a derived name (Stockpile `stockpile1`..`3`, Protosynthesis and Quark Drive `protosynthesisatk`) is matched to its condition id by dropping a trailing number or stat id (`atk def spa spd spe`), in both readers. Limits: the engine's `onCopy` hooks are not modelled (Gastro Acid is dropped silently when the incoming Pokemon's ability cannot be suppressed; Power Trick/Shift stat swaps are internal), and a `transform` volatile the reducer records is passed like any other id although the engine's receiver is not transformed. `fixtures/showdown/<format>/baton-pass-volatiles/battle.json` (the same script's second battle: Substitute + Baton Pass users, found when a Substitute is handed on), `tests/ml/reducer/test_baton_pass_volatiles.py`, parity and `packages/public-state/test/baton-pass.test.ts`. Reducer sweep (#919, 350 seeded battles, three mixes, `--only-move 'Baton Pass,Shed Tail'` on the committed fixtures dataset): `unexplained: 0`. Fling of an item that is already gone (#1162): the engine's Fling ends the thrown item in the move's own `onUpdate`, after the hit, and reads the holder's item then; when the hit started a terrain through Seed Sower and a Grassy Seed was eaten at once, `getItem()` names nothing and the line is `|-enditem|POKEMON||[from] move: Fling` (run public-main-010, generation 19, game 390: `VocabularyError: unknown item ''`). The reducer and the interpreter accept an empty item name only with that exact `[from]` tag (the holder ends with no item, lost; the engine's `setItem('')`), and any other empty name is still refused. `fixtures/showdown/<format>/fling/battle.json` (`scripts/capture-fling-battle.ts`), `tests/ml/reducer/test_fling.py`. Known, unchanged: a `-boost ... [from] item: Grassy Seed` that follows the seed's `-enditem` sets the holder's `item` back to the seed (with `item_lost` kept true) until a later line ends it. A repeated `[spread]` tag is accepted (the engine's `attrLastMove` appends one per call that reaches the move line, e.g. Magic Bounce reflecting a spread move; the last value is kept), every other repeated tag with a value still fails closed. Frisk (#1171): `|-item|TARGET|ITEM|[from] ability: Frisk|[of] FRISKER` is the target's item (as before) and now also reveals the Frisk holder's ability (`frisk` on the `[of]` Pokemon; before, no reader credited it to anyone), in the reducer and the shared interpreter alike (`tests/ml/reducer/test_corpus.py`, `packages/public-state/test/lines.test.ts` over every committed Frisk reveal); this changes `readerSha256` (no committed vector pins it: the damage-aware and feature-set identity hashes are recomputed from it, so a run or record made before this change names the old reader hash; `config/search/rerank-1.v1.json` names the belief identity hash, and was edited in place, since no run has used it) and the replay pages' hashed script. `-copyboost|A|B` sets A's boosts from B's (the engine's Psych Up and Costar code; sim/SIM-PROTOCOL.md says the reverse). The #919 sweep (5,500 seeded battles on the generated dataset, five actor mixes) found these two and no other refusal. A player-alone `|error|[Unavailable choice] <text>` (the engine's refusal of a pending candidate, #909; on A2 also `[Invalid choice] <text>` from the server layer) changes no state and the text is dropped; any other `|error|` shape is a `MalformedLineError`. `-zbroken` (a Z-move breaking Protect, champions mod) changes no state. Tests: `tests/ml/reducer/test_unavailable.py` over `fixtures/showdown/<format>/unavailable/` (seeded pending-first battles that reach the refusal, with reduced snapshots `reduced/unavailable-*.json`).

**Recent history** (#1013, ADR-010 erratum A4 item 7; `state.py` `Recent`, `reducer.py`; `STATE_SCHEMA_VERSION` 2). The reduced state now carries what happened lately, from the player's own channel only; the reducer's state is still not part of `encoder_version` and only the encoder option `recent_history` (#1014, below) consumes the fields, so without it the encoder, checkpoints and policy ids are unchanged, but `to_dict` gained keys, so every `reduced/*.json` golden was rewritten (additions only; the replay interpreter does not carry them and the parity test strips `recent`, `condition_turns` and `elapsed`). Per Pokemon `recent` (reset on switching out or fainting, carried by an Illusion `_settle`): `active_turns` (`|turn|` lines since the switch-in, as the world facts' `active_turns`), `last_move` and `last_target` (`own:a` / `opponent:b`: the first target the `|move|` line prints, so the first foe of a spread move; null when the line prints none or `null`) with `last_move_turn` (a `|move|` without `[from]`, `[from]lockedmove` included, as the world facts' `last_move`; Round/Dancer-style action sources are not counted here because the reducer holds no such list), `last_action_turn`/`last_action_order` (the place among the turn's move and `|cant|` lines, from 1), `hp_taken_*` and `hp_dealt_*` for `this_turn` and `last_turn` (the sum of `-damage` decreases, in the units the player sees: exact for an own Pokemon, points of 100 for an opponent; damage dealt is in the TARGET's units, so `hp_dealt_*` is NOT one scale: percent when the Pokemon hit a foe, exact own HP when a foe hit an own Pokemon (an ally hit by a spread move counts too, in the ally's units), and #1014 must not treat it as one scale; an opponent's `hp_dealt_*` is therefore own exact HP, which the canary helper `leaks` does not scan; dealt is the untagged damage after a `|move|`, anything with `[from]` is taken but dealt by nobody; heals and `-sethp` are not counted) and `protect_streak` (consecutive turns of successful stalling uses, as the world facts' `protect_streak`: `-singleturn` of the user after its move succeeds, `-fail` ends it; the engine prints one of the two after every stalling `|move|`, so a stalling move still waiting at the next `|move|`, `|cant|`, switch, `|turn|`, `|win|` or `|tie|` is a `MalformedLineError`, as the world facts' `stall_unresolved`; one silence is the engine's own, #1281: a stalling move whose target is the user's own side, Quick Guard or Wide Guard, prints NEITHER line when its side's condition is already up this turn, because `Side.addSideCondition` then returns false without a message while the move's `onHitSide` still adds the user's `stall` volatile and the move does not fail (`sim/battle-actions.ts` `moveHit`; stopped run public-main-df3c-002 at generation 21, game 747, two Pokemon of one side both using Wide Guard); the reducer counts that use as a success for the streak, but only for a move whose engine-data target is `allySide` (`Vocabulary.ally_side_moves`, from the hashed engine-data export, so no data file or hash moved) and only after an earlier `-singleturn` of the same move on the same side this turn (`_guards`, cleared at `|turn|`); any other silence still raises. The world facts take the same rule through the optional `WorldFactsData.ally_side_moves` (the stall moves with target `allySide`, `readWorldFactsData`), without a `WORLD_FACTS_FORMAT` bump since only a former refusal gets a value; `fixtures/showdown/<format>/side-guard/battle.json` (`scripts/capture-side-guard-battle.ts`), `tests/ml/reducer/test_side_guard.py`, the parity sweep and its oracle). Instructed stalling move (#1377): Instruct re-runs the user's last move in the turn it already ran; the engine passes `onPrepareHit` and `onHit` restarts the `stall` volatile (counter x3), but the move's own volatile is still up, so `addVolatile` returns false and `onStart` prints no `-singleturn` (a failed stall roll still prints `-fail`). A stalling `|move|` by a Pokemon whose stalling use already succeeded THIS turn is therefore a silent success counted as one more use (no move name in code). Only the Python reducer takes it: the TS world facts and history facts (and `history.py`, their twin) still refuse it as `stall_unresolved`, because editing those files moves their file hashes, which are part of the probe-outcome/1 and damage-aware identities; aligning them with the engine is batched with #1375, beside the #1281 side-guard silence. Which moves stall is engine data: `ml/areneon_ml/reducer/data/stall-moves.<format>.json` (`stall_moves_hash`, `packages/showdown-bridge/src/stall-moves.ts`, `scripts/export-stall-moves.ts` after a pin bump, `test/stall-moves.test.ts` re-exports and compares byte for byte and holds it equal to the world facts' `stall_moves`); `Vocabulary.stall_moves` is None for a pin the file was not exported for, and then EVERY `|move|` (not only a stalling one) raises `VocabularyError`: re-export after a pin bump (`read_stall_moves` refuses an unknown key, a hash mismatch, another format or schema version, as `read_volatile_copy`). Field: `elapsed` (`weather`, `terrain`, `field_conditions` by id) and per side `condition_turns` are the turns since each began (0: this turn; the first layer's turn; a `[upkeep]` weather line starts nothing; `-swapsideconditions` swaps them); the engine's `after_residual` nuance of the world facts is not modelled. Tests: `tests/ml/reducer/test_recent.py` (hand-written logs for every field, the canary and its negative controls).

**Encoder option `recent_history`** (#1014, ADR-010 erratum A4 item 7; `model/encoder.py`, `model/options.py`; `model.encoder.options` of `TrainingRunConfig` and `CloneRunConfig`, only `true`, layout changing, checkpoint format `"2"`). The Pokemon and field tokens also read the reducer's recent history; no network code changed (the Battle and Preview networks build their input layers from the layout). Per Pokemon: a category `last_move` (move vocabulary, NONE before any move) after the move columns, and numbers after the volatile buckets: `active_turns`, `last_move_age`, `last_action_known`, `last_action_age` (turns before the current one, over `turn_scale`), `last_action_order` (over 8), `hp_taken_*` and `hp_dealt_*` for this and last turn, `protect_streak` (over 4) and a one-hot `last_target_<own|opponent>_<a|b|c>`. Field: `weather_elapsed`, `terrain_elapsed` and bucketed `field_turns_i`, `own_side_turns_i`, `opponent_side_turns_i` (turns since each condition began, over `turn_scale`, summed in the condition's bucket). **Units (`Recent` sums are not one scale):** damage taken is in the units of the Pokemon's own side (own: exact hit points over `stat_scale`; opponent: points of 100 over 100); damage dealt is in its target's units, which the sum does not say, so it is read in the units of the usual target: an own Pokemon's over 100 (a foe), an opponent's over `stat_scale` (an own Pokemon); an ally hit by a spread move adds its units to the sum (a known imprecision, not split because that needs separate counters in the reducer). The divisors that are not config fields (100, 8, 4, the slot-letter count and the unit rule) are hashed into `encoder_version`; absent the option the version, tensors and golden digests are today's (pinned). Tests: `tests/ml/model/test_recent_history.py` (hand-computed values on a fixed query, A1/A2 equality on the committed pairs, composition with `candidate_matchup`, `candidate_target` and both feature sets, the network and the checkpoint).

Tests (`tests/ml/reducer/`): a unit test per line type, the refusals, the canary with negative controls, a drift test of the request key sets against `engine-request.schema.json`, a corpus test over `fixtures/showdown/<format>/random/` (seeded random-legal vs random-legal battles on random synthetic teams, `random-corpus.ts`; CI re-plays each from its committed seed, teams and actor seeds) that requires zero refusals (Illusion's `|replace|` included), with the canary at every request point, `test_illusion.py` (hand-written logs for every Illusion branch, 100% on the reducer) and `test_illusion_battle.py` over `fixtures/showdown/<format>/illusion/battle.json` (a seeded random-legal battle with a Zoroark on both sides whose disguises break, `scripts/capture-illusion-battle.ts`; its canary: before the replace nothing of the real Pokemon is in the state), and golden snapshots `fixtures/showdown/<format>/reduced/p1.json` / `p2.json` for every committed channel (and `illusion-p1.json` / `illusion-p2.json`) (rewrite deliberately with `ARENEON_UPDATE_GOLDEN=1 uv run pytest tests/ml/reducer/test_golden.py --no-cov` and review the diff).

**Encoder, candidate parser, networks, checkpoints and the C1/C6 checker** (`ml/areneon_ml/model/`, `ml/areneon_ml/checkpoint/`, ADR-010 §3, §5, #839). `GameData.from_table(engine_data)` indexes the hashed engine-data export: one `IdVocab` per vocabulary (species, moves, items, abilities, statuses, weathers, terrains, types; index 0 is none, 1 unknown, the engine's ids follow in the export's order) and the numeric attribute tables (types, base stats, weight; move power, accuracy, priority, PP, target and flag one-hots; item flags), each column scaled by the largest value in the data. No list of game content is written in model code, so a vocabulary grows with the table. `Encoder(game, EncoderConfig).encode(query)` reads only `query["observation"]` (`player`, `kind`, `log`, `team.team`) and `query["candidates"]` (`choice`, `pending`): the log goes through `reduce_channel`, the player's last `|request|` gives the Pokémon order (`switch N` / `team NNNN` name the Pokémon at that request position) and the move ids, and it returns tensors without a batch dimension (`EncodedQuery`: one token row per own then opponent Pokémon, the field features, per-candidate slot parts, the picked team tokens and the `pending` flag). It ignores `decision_context`, `identity` (so `rqid`), the `rqid` inside `|request|` lines and every verdict field, so the A1 and A2 forms of a decision give equal tensors (tests over every request of the `fixtures/a2/` recordings and the `policy-query` samples). Volatiles and field/side conditions are engine-owned ids with no vocabulary yet, so they are SHA-256 bucketed into `volatile_buckets` / `condition_buckets`. `encoder_version` is `enc<format>:` + SHA-256 of the engine-data hash, the attribute tables as built, the config (scales included), the feature layout and the index constants (genders, request kinds, events, target codes, slot letters, the bucket hash), so a pin bump or any such change is a new version, never a silent remap. A change in how a value is computed from the state, with the same names and constants, is not visible to the hash: bump `ENCODER_FORMAT`; a golden test pins one version string and the tensor digests of fixed queries and fails on such a change. The A1/A2 equality is also tested on `fixtures/showdown/<format>/a2-pairs.json` (the A1 replay's observations and the A2 frames' observations of the ADR-005 fixtures with candidates from the real `enumerateCandidates`; rewrite with `node packages/bot-client/scripts/capture-encoder-pairs.ts`, a bot-client test compares it with a fresh capture). The encoder raises (fails closed) when an observation's kind does not match its request or candidates, or a Pokémon shows more moves than `max_moves`. `parse_choice` (`model/candidates.py`, re-exported by the worker) splits a canonical candidate into `SlotAction`s (pass, move with optional target and event, switch) or a Team Preview order and refuses any other shape with `UnsupportedCandidate`; `ParsedChoice.format()` is its exact inverse, and `tests/ml/model/test_candidates.py` round-trips every candidate of every `fixtures/showdown/**/choices/*.json`. `CheckpointRecord` (`packages/schema/contracts/v1/checkpoint-record.schema.json`, generated TS and pydantic types, corpus samples in `fixtures/contracts/v1/checkpoint-record/`) is the content-addressed provenance record of one frozen checkpoint; `checkpoint/record.py` reads it (sorted and unique hash arrays, no id referring to the record itself). Part 2 (networks and checkpoints, #839). `PolicyNetwork(role, game, ModelConfig)` (`model/network.py`) is one architecture per role, built and stored separately: a transformer over the entity tokens plus a field token and an aggregation token (attention written out as matmul and softmax, no fused kernel), a candidate head (a `battle` candidate embeds its two slot actions, move, target, event, acting Pokémon and switch-in tokens; a `preview` candidate its ordered team positions; both add the pending flag, cross-attend over the state and score against the aggregation token) and a value head on the aggregation token. Every size is configuration (`ModelConfig`: `d_model`, layers, heads, `d_ff` and the nested `EncoderConfig`; there are no defaults: the `model` section of the run's `TrainingRunConfig` carries the values and code only bounds them), same-seed initialisation is claimed for one machine and software only, vocabulary sizes come from `GameData`, and attribute tables are buffers derived from the engine data, not parameters. `collate` pads queries into a `Batch` (training may batch); evaluation runs one query per forward pass, a batch of one with no padding, on CPU with `configure_cpu_inference()` (deterministic algorithms, one thread), and `tests/ml/model/test_network.py` shows a query's logits are bit-identical alone and after others, and equal for the A1 and A2 forms. A checkpoint (`checkpoint/files.py`) is one safetensors file: the tensors, and `__metadata__` with the format, role, model config, encoder version and engine-data hash. The writer is our own so the bytes are deterministic (the library orders metadata by hash-map iteration); the id is the SHA-256 of the bytes; `load_checkpoint(data, game, expected_id, role)` (the id is required and checked before anything is read; the configuration is bounded, so a crafted file cannot allocate without limit) refuses bytes that do not hash to the id, another role, engine-data table or encoder version, an invalid configuration, and tensors whose names, dtype or shape differ from the network's. No pickle and no `torch.load` anywhere (a syntactic AST scan of all of `ml/areneon_ml` (a guard, not a proof: it misses aliased and dynamic imports) bans imports of pickle, dill, cloudpickle, shelve, marshal and numpy and calls of `torch.load` / `torch.jit.load`). numpy is not in the closure: the writer and reader move bytes through `ctypes`/`torch.frombuffer`. `python -m areneon_ml.checkpoint init --role … --seed … --engine-data … --run-dir … --model-config f` (the `model` section of a `TrainingRunConfig`, required) writes a seeded, untrained checkpoint (generation 0, and the input of the worker tests); learner state and training are in [training-loop-learner.md](training-loop-learner.md) (#840). `checkpoint/provenance.py` is the C1/C6 checker (`ProvenanceGraph(records, sources)`; the `sources` registry is derived from the run's own manifests (#853: `sources_registry` / `population_sources` in `loop/driver.py`) and hash-bound: `loop provenance` and `learner clone` write `provenance.json` beside `sources.json` (`checkpoint/binding.py`: the registry's SHA-256 and the SHA-256 of every record file by checkpoint id, canonical JSON), and `python -m areneon_ml.checkpoint classify|independent --records <dir> --sources <json>` refuses (exit 3) a registry without that binding, a registry that differs from it, a changed, added or dropped record, or a non-canonical file; `--run-dir R` instead derives records and registry from the run again (all manifests verified; no hand input at all). Both print the registry's hash so an evaluation plan can bind it. The printed result names its `basis`: `run-dir` (authoritative; an evaluation plan pins the `sources_hash` computed this way) or `exported-binding`, which is only a self-consistency check (a single-file edit or corrupted copy is caught; someone who rewrites both `sources.json` and `provenance.json` is not), and `import-clone` verifies the clone output's binding before trusting its `sources.json`. Records are keyed by their own `checkpoint_id` (`record_files`; two files naming one id are refused). Not covered: ADR-010 §4 L2 separation of run key, init seed and team draws): the ancestry of a checkpoint is itself plus everything reachable through `parent`, `league_opponents`, `exploiter_target` and the generators of its training sets (the `sources` registry maps each training-set hash to the checkpoints that generated it; an empty list is an outside behaviour-cloning or fine-tuning source, and a set missing from the registry is refused); C6 independence is disjoint ancestries (learned checkpoints and data sources; scripted baselines and the team pool are not nodes), C1 calls an opponent `seen` when it is in the candidate's ancestry, a scripted baseline, or a related lineage, and `held_out` only when every opponent checkpoint is an evaluation exploiter of the candidate or an independent lineage (one related member of a Preview + Battle pair makes the pair `seen`). The records must agree with each other: a parent shares its child's encoder version, a parent shares the child's role (a warm start cannot cross architectures), league opponents and the exploiter target may be either half of a frozen Preview + Battle pair, and nothing synthetic descends from a private checkpoint. It fails closed: a referenced checkpoint without a record, a repeated record, a cycle, or an exploiter in its own target's league (C3) is a `ProvenanceError`.

**Model options** (`ml/areneon_ml/model/options.py`, ADR-010 erratum A4 item 2, #1002; the base of the architecture arms of Epic #1000). `model.options` (network) and `model.encoder.options` (encoder) are optional closed objects of the `TrainingRunConfig` (a minor change within v1; `EncoderConfig.options` and `ModelConfig.options` hold them as sorted tuples). **Absent keeps today's bytes**: `as_dict` omits the key when no option is set (an empty object counts as none for the bytes and `encoder_version`; its run config hash still differs from an absent key), so `encoder_version`, checkpoint bytes and ids, learner state hashes and the layout of every existing record are unchanged (tests pin a checkpoint id and the encoder version computed before the change). Present, an option is part of the run config hash, `encoder_version` (encoder options, through the config) and the checkpoint's `model_config` metadata. `REGISTRY` (per scope, `OptionSpec(name, check, layout_changing)`) holds the options below, starting with the encoder option `candidate_matchup`; any other name is refused, and each further arm's ticket adds its spec and its schema property (tests register test-only specs with `monkeypatch`). **`candidate_matchup`** (ADR-022 tier B, ADR-010 erratum A5 item 1, #1023; `"model": {"encoder": {..., "options": {"candidate_matchup": true}}}`, only `true` is a value, layout changing): a battle candidate's move slot actions gain seven categorical columns after the five base ones (`BASE_CAND_COLUMNS`): for each opposing position and the ally, the engine-data type-chart cell (`GameData.type_chart`, the export's `types[].damage_taken` code) of the move's type against each of the position's first two shown types, and a same-type flag (the move's type is one of the acting Pokemon's shown types). A cell is `RESERVED` + the export's code (so the network embeds the engine's own codes; no multiplier, product of types or same-type bonus value is written anywhere, and a source scan in `tests/ml/model/test_matchup.py` holds that), `NONE` for an empty or fainted position, a missing second type, a status move, a switch or a pass. Only the species a position shows enters (`temp_species` or `species`, else the roster's base species): never an unrevealed item, ability or move, and a type change by a volatile is not tracked. The format has no Terastallisation (the reducer refuses a `-terastallize` line). The Battle network embeds the columns (`matchup`, `same_type` embeddings, wider `slot_parts`); the Preview network ignores them. With the option absent the layout, `encoder_version`, tensors and checkpoint ids are today's (pinned tests); with it the config and layout move `encoder_version` and `GameData` gained the chart tables (not part of `attribute_digest`). The checkpoint `format` is `"2"` (`FORMAT_OPTIONS`) only for a config with a `layout_changing` option and `"1"` otherwise; `load_checkpoint` refuses a format that does not fit the file's options and, given `configured` (the caller's `ModelConfig`), a file whose network or encoder options differ (`CheckpointOptionsError`, a `CheckpointFileError`); the worker passes the model section of `<run-dir>/run-config.json` as `configured` when that file exists (a hand-made run directory without it is checked by the checkpoint alone; exit 4 on a mismatch or an unusable section). The learner-state sidecar carries `model_options` (`network` / `encoder`) only when an option is set, and `restore_state` refuses any difference, in either direction, with `LearnerOptionsError` (a `LearnerStateError`) before the run config hash check. The `clone-run-config` contract takes the same optional `model.options` and `model.encoder.options` objects (#1168; same rules, absent keeps the record's bytes and `clone_run_config_hash`; present flows through `ModelConfig.from_dict` into the clone's networks, `encoder_version`, checkpoint metadata and format), so a clone made with `candidate_matchup` warm-starts only a run that sets it and a run without it (or the reverse) refuses the clone at generation 0 (`tests/ml/loop/test_warm_start_clone_options.py`).

**Encoder option `exact_volatiles`** (#1019, ADR-010 erratum A4 item 7; `model/encoder.py` `exact_ids`, `Layout.of(config, game)`, `reducer/vocab.py` `resolve_volatile`; `model.encoder.options` of `TrainingRunConfig` and `CloneRunConfig`, only `true`, layout changing, checkpoint format `"2"`). Volatiles and field and side conditions are exact vocabulary ids instead of SHA-256 buckets: one column per id of `exact_ids(game)`, the sorted union of the version 2 table's `conditions`, `PROTOCOL_VOLATILES` and the move, ability and item ids (the engine prints a volatile, field or side condition under the name of what started it), the same width for the Pokemon's `volatile_<i>` columns and the field token's three condition blocks (and the `recent_history` `*_turns_<i>` blocks, when both are set). A volatile resolves through `Vocabulary.resolve_volatile` (the id, or the id without its derived number or stat suffix). **Derived suffixes** follow the engine's printed sequence, read from the pinned `data/moves.ts` and `abilities.ts`: Stockpile prints `stockpile1`..`3` with no `-end` between layers and ends as `-end|Stockpile` (bare name), Perish Song prints `perish3`..`perish0` counting down with no `-end`, Supreme Overlord `fallenN` and `-end|fallenN`, Protosynthesis and Quark Drive `protosynthesis<stat>` and `-end|Protosynthesis`. The raw ids in `PokemonState.volatiles` therefore accumulate or go stale, so the reducer keeps the truth in the new bookkeeping field `PokemonState.derived` (condition id to its latest suffix, v2 tables only; set on `-start`, dropped on `-end` of the base id or the numbered id, cleared on switching out, carried by an Illusion settle and, on a Baton Pass, for the raw ids the copy table passes (Perish Song's countdown passes; Stockpile and Protosynthesis are `noCopy`; known limit: Supreme Overlord's `fallenN` also passes, raw id and `derived["fallen"]` alike, although its count lives in the ability's own state in the engine, pinned by a test so a later fix is deliberate); not in `to_dict`, so no snapshot, parity or reader hash changed). The encoder skips the raw numbered ids and reads `derived`: a number n gives `1 + n / DERIVED_SCALE` (8, hashed) in the id's column (`stockpile` 1-3, `perish` 3-0, `fallen` 1-5), a stat suffix gives 1 and sets that stat's `boosted_stat_<atk|def|spa|spd|spe>` flag on the Pokemon token; side conditions keep their layer counts, the field is 1. **Width and memory:** each of the volatile, field, own-side and opponent-side groups is about 1.5-2k columns (`len(exact_ids)`), against 4096 buckets at the default config, but the one-hot-like inputs are sparse; with `recent_history` the field token carries three more groups of that width. A Pokemon token also gets `types_known`, `types_typeless` and one `type_<id>` flag per type for the reducer's `types` (set by a `typechange`; an empty list from the copy form sets only `types_known`). **Needs a version 2 engine-data table**: `Layout.of`, `encoder_version`, `Encoder` and `init_network` refuse a version 1 table with an `EncodingError` ("needs a version 2 engine-data table"), and an id the vocabulary does not resolve is an `EncodingError` (fails closed). `Layout.of(config, game=None)` takes the game only for this option (everything else is unchanged; `network.py` passes it). Absent keeps `Layout`, `encoder_version`, tensors and checkpoint ids (pinned). `encoder_version` hashes the id list, the protocol words and the value rules. **Which table:** the worker, learner and loop load the `--engine-data` file they are given, so a run with the option passes `fixtures/showdown/<format>/engine-data-2.json`; the checkpoint's engine-data hash check refuses any other table. The TS commands that read `--engine-data` only for its hash (`generation-cli`, `progress-cli`, `private-test-cli`) now accept either version (`readEngineDataAny`, by `schema_version`). Tests: `tests/ml/reducer/test_derived.py` (`derived` after start, end, switch-out, faint, Baton Pass, Shed Tail, Illusion settle), `tests/ml/model/test_exact_volatiles.py` (hand-computed volatile, type and condition columns, the refusals, A1/A2 pairs, a random-corpus slice, composition with `candidate_matchup`, `candidate_target`, `recent_history` and `candidate_features`, the network and checkpoint, golden digests of fixed queries).

**`candidate_target`** (encoder option, ADR-010 erratum A4 item 7, #1011; `model.encoder.options: {"candidate_target": true}`, only `true` is a value, layout changing, checkpoint format `"2"`, both `TrainingRunConfig` and `CloneRunConfig` accept it). Each battle move part gains a third token column (`Layout.cand_tok` is `("actor", "switch_in", "target")`, `cand_tok` [N, SLOTS, 3]): the token of the Pokemon the move's target location names, read in the engine's convention (`getAtLoc`: L > 0 is the foe in opposing active slot L - 1, token `n_own` + its roster index; L < 0 is the own active slot -L - 1, the own token at that request position), -1 for an untargeted or spread move, the user itself, a switch, a pass, an empty or fainted slot. The Battle network gathers that token's embedding (`_gather(mons, tok[..., 2])`) as one more d-wide slot-part input; the Preview network ignores it. Absent keeps `Layout.as_dict`, `encoder_version`, tensors and checkpoint ids (`cand_tok` stays width 2; the golden version is pinned); it composes with `candidate_matchup` and `candidate_features` (independent columns). `collate` refuses queries of two token layouts and the Battle network a batch of another. `src/choice-channels.ts` replays the committed choice corpus on the pinned engine into the fixture `choice-channels/channels.json` (`scripts/capture-choice-channels.ts`; `test/choice-channels.test.ts` compares it with a fresh replay; the Python `tests/ml/model/test_target.py` reads only the fixture and checks every corpus candidate against an independent species mapping read from the log and request).

**`factored_preview`** (network option, ADR-010 erratum A4 item 4, #1007; `model.options: {"factored_preview": true}`, only `true` is a value, layout changing, checkpoint format `"2"`; both `TrainingRunConfig` and `CloneRunConfig` accept it, the first NETWORK option in `REGISTRY["network"]`). The Preview network scores an order as `sum over places p of u(place p, token p) + v(token at place 1, token at place 2)` (`place_score` and `lead_score`, each Linear(2d, d), GELU, Linear(d, 1); the place embedding `place` is kept; order kept, `max_picks` at least 2 or `ConfigError`). The candidate projection, `pending`, the two candidate norms, the cross-attention and the interaction `score` are not built for Preview under the option (so the pending flag is not read there; the value head is unchanged); candidate set, order and the masked softmax over real candidates are unchanged. The Battle network ignores the option (same tensors and logits). Absent keeps every Preview tensor name and logit (`test_factored_preview.py` pins the names and the existing golden digests pin the logits; `test_options.py` only updates the registry assertion). `tests/ml/model/test_factored_preview.py`: the logits equal a loop-computed sum on the trunk's tokens, a zeroed-term check, bit-identical alone and after other queries, A1/A2 forms equal, padding, and the checkpoint loads only under the option.

**`separate_value` and `policy_output_scale_ppm`** (network options, ADR-010 erratum A4 item 7, #1016; both under `model.options` of `TrainingRunConfig` and `CloneRunConfig`, layout changing, checkpoint format `"2"`; absent keeps every tensor name, checkpoint id and logit, pinned by the existing tests). `separate_value` is `"network"` or `"detached"` (a closed set; the model-options reader now takes string values). `"network"`: `PolicyNetwork(..., value_only=True)` is built as `value_net.*` (its own embeddings, token layers, blocks, final norm, probe layers if the layout has them, and value head; the candidate and scorer parts are not built) and the outer network has no `value.*`; same encoder inputs and the config's sizes, no shared layer or storage, it is built AFTER all the policy modules (its construction draws from the seeded RNG last, so the policy's initial weights and logits are identical to the plain network's for a seed; tested). `"network"` roughly doubles the inference cost of a forward (a second full trunk runs for the value; the logits do not need it, but the value does), which matters for latency budgets and the search leaf; `"detached"` costs nothing extra. `"detached"`: the head stays `value.*` on the trunk output with `detach()`, so tensor names and outputs are unchanged and only the gradient differs. Policy loss never reaches the value network and value loss never the policy trunk or scorer (gradient tests, both roles); one Adam serves both since the gradients are disjoint, and the value warm-up trains the `value.` and `value_net.` prefixes (`VALUE_PREFIXES`). `policy_output_scale_ppm` is an integer 1 to 999999 (10000 is the arm's 1/100; 1,000,000 is refused): after the default init, the weight of the scorer's last layer (`score.2`, or `place_score.2` and `lead_score.2` under `factored_preview`) is multiplied by `value / 10^6` (bias and value head untouched; initial weights only, no tensor name changes), so a seeded network's initial logit spread is 1/100 of the plain one's (test, ratio between 99 and 101). It composes with `factored_preview` and `separate_value` (tests). Tests: `tests/ml/model/test_value_scale.py` (including a rich encoder with probe-damage/2, recent history and candidate matchup, and `NetworkScorer.evaluate`), `tests/ml/learner/test_value_warmup.py` (both modes), `test_options.py` (state restore refusals both ways), `test_clone.py` (one-epoch clone with the options), corpus samples `*-run-config/{valid/separate-value,valid/policy-output-scale,invalid/separate-value-unknown,invalid/policy-output-scale-default}`.

**`shared_trunk`** (network option, ADR-010 erratum A4 item 5, #1009; `model.options.shared_trunk: true` of `TrainingRunConfig` and `CloneRunConfig`, layout changing so checkpoint format `"2"`; absent keeps every checkpoint, learner-state and policy-id byte). The Preview and Battle networks use ONE embedding and transformer trunk: `PolicyNetwork.trunk_keys()` names it (the embeddings and attribute projections of the vocabulary kinds the token and field columns read, `mon_cat`, `mon_num`, `side`, `field_cat`, `field_num`, `aggregate`, `blocks`, `final_norm`); the candidate parts, probe layers, candidate/score heads, value head and a `separate_value: "network"` value network (`value_net.*`, its own trunk by definition) stay role-specific, so `separate_value` combines with it. `Learner.__init__` ties the Preview network's trunk modules to the Battle network's (`tie_trunk`: the very same parameters; the Battle network's seeded trunk is the shared one, the Preview's own seeded trunk is dropped), builds ONE Adam over the union of the two networks' parameters (each shared parameter once; `Learner.entries`, `optimiser_groups()`), and clips the gradient norm over that union. Joint step (only under the option; without it the per-role loop and every byte are as before): both roles' targets (frozen log-probabilities, values, GAE) are read from the pre-update weights first, then each epoch shuffles each role's steps with the learner generator (Battle's draw, then Preview's) and cuts both into the SAME number M of minibatches: the leading role (Battle, else Preview) by `ppo.minibatch_size`, the other spread as evenly as possible over the M (`Learner._spread`: the first `len % M` chunks hold one more step; a role with fewer steps than M fills the first minibatches only, and a missing part contributes 0; Preview's chunks may exceed `minibatch_size`). Each optimiser step backpropagates `loss_battle_mb + loss_preview_mb` (each a mean over its own decisions in that minibatch, the Battle-only anchor term and the value-only warm-up loss included in the sum), clips over the union and steps the one Adam, so the trunk gradient is the sum of both roles' gradients and Preview's ratio, clip fraction and `approx_kl` are measured against the trunk both roles move. `RoleReport.optimiser_steps` counts the joint steps a role contributed to; `ppo.target_kl_micro` is checked once per complete joint epoch and stops BOTH roles when the epoch-mean `approx_kl` of any non-warm role exceeds it (`epochs_run` and `kl_stopped` are then equal in both reports). A checkpoint is still one complete network per role, the trunk tensors written into both and equal; `check_shared_trunk(pair)` (`network.py`) refuses a pair whose trunk tensors differ or where only one network has the option (`TrunkError`), and `NetworkScorer` calls it (the worker exits with the checkpoint code); the loop's resume re-verifies the weights against the stored checkpoint ids. Learner state: the sidecar's `model_options` records the option, so a restore under the other layout is a `LearnerOptionsError` in both directions; the Adam moments of the trunk are stored once under the Battle role (`opt.battle.*`; the Preview role stores only its own heads), the weights stay complete per role (`net.preview.<trunk>` too) and `restore_state` refuses a file whose two trunk copies differ (`LearnerStateError`). **Decisions:** (1) A `{preview, battle}` `warm_start` is refused with the option (both readers: two cloned trunks conflict), but a Battle-only `warm_start: {battle}` (with `ppo.value_warmup` and `ppo.anchor`) is supported: at generation 0 the Preview network is tied to the CLONED Battle network's trunk and keeps its heads from the seeded `seeds.init_preview`, so Preview's generation-0 checkpoint is a new file (its id differs from a scratch run's) with an ordinary built record (no training sets, no parent, this run's state hash), the Battle record stays the clone record byte for byte, and `load_warm_start` / `import-clone` check the Battle clone as before; the clone must have been made with the same `shared_trunk` option (the checkpoint's model section must equal the run's), so `CloneRunConfig` accepts it too (a clone still trains its two roles separately, so a two-role clone pair never has equal trunks, and only its Battle half is usable). During the value warm-up the whole shared trunk is frozen (it is the warm Battle role's policy trunk): only `value.*` of Battle trains, and the unwarmed Preview role trains its own heads (candidate parts and value head) with the trunk frozen; after the warm-up both roles train normally. The KL anchor applies to the Battle role only (the warm roles, as without the option) and its gradient reaches the shared trunk, which is intended (`tests/ml/loop/test_shared_trunk_warm.py`: trunk equal in both files each generation, trunk unchanged through the warm-up, rerun and kills at four stages, provenance export); (2) a clone trains the roles separately, so the option does not make a jointly trained clone; (3) `python -m areneon_ml.checkpoint init` refuses it (one role alone has no partner trunk; the generation loop makes the pair); (5) with `opponent_sheet` the `sheet_other` embedding is trunk (`trunk_keys` names it) and `tie_trunk` ties it, so a Battle-only warm start's generation 0 passes `check_shared_trunk` (it was untied: the worker refused the pair); (4) the Preview network's `probe-damage/2` summaries do not exist (only the Battle network reads probe rows), so the shared trunk is fed different inputs by the two roles. Tests: `tests/ml/learner/test_shared_trunk.py`, `tests/ml/loop/test_shared_trunk.py` (rerun and kills at four stages reach identical learner-state hashes), the corpus samples `training-run-config/shared-trunk`, `-false` and the reader-invalid `shared-trunk-warm-start`.

**Incremental load** (#958): `learner/data.py` `trajectories` reduces each Episode's channel once instead of re-reducing every example's log from its start. An `Encoder.session()` (`EncoderSession`) feeds a `ChannelReducer` (the reducer driven line by line; `reduce_channel` is a loop over it) only the lines it has not seen and encodes at once, while the live state equals `reduce_channel`'s for that prefix, and `worker/view.py` `IncrementalView` strips `rqid` from each line once. A log that does not extend the lines already fed restarts the reduction, so the tensors always equal `Encoder.encode(encoder_input(query))` (`tests/ml/learner/test_incremental.py` over every committed battle channel; the encoder version and `ENCODER_FORMAT` are unchanged). The remaining load cost is `read_stream`'s per-example contract validation of the whole stream.
