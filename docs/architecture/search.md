# Decision-time search

World facts, the scenario world builder, the fork oracle, search worlds, the probe reply model and the `rerank-1` search actor.

Part of the architecture documentation split out of `CLAUDE.md` (index: [README](README.md)). Ticket references such as `#1179` point to Redmine (`redmine.piglor.com/projects/areneon`), where measured figures and verdicts are recorded.

**World facts** (`packages/public-state/src/world-facts.ts`, ADR-026 §1 and §9 slice L, #1136). The facts the reviewed world writers need and the shared reader does not track, derived from ONE player's own channel by a module of its own: `WorldFactsReader` (`feed` a line, `facts()` at any point), `worldFacts` and `worldFactsAtRequests` (the facts before each `|request|` line, a refusal counted where it stands). It imports nothing (a test enforces it) and has its own `WORLD_FACTS_FORMAT` (`world-facts/4`, bumped when a fact is computed differently or when a channel that was refused now yields facts; part of the search identity, ADR-026 §5; `/4` is #1282: #1281 made a second Wide Guard or Quick Guard on one side, silent in the engine, a success where it raised `stall_unresolved`, so a decision refused before (`facts_unavailable`, search fallback) now builds worlds and searches, and the same search identity would have named different play. No fact of an already accepted channel changed value. The search identity test vector moved (Python hashlib over the sorted-key compact JSON of the thirteen inputs; the policy-id vectors take a search hash as an opaque string and are unchanged), and no committed config embeds the search identity, so `config/search/rerank-1.v1.json` and the contract samples did not move) and file hash (`worldFactsSha256()`, in `index.ts`); `interpreter.cjs`, `readerSha256()` and every replay page are unchanged. The input is a branded `OwnChannel` that only `ownChannel(player, lines)` makes (a bad player, a line that is no protocol line and a `|split|` marker are refused), so an omniscient log, a team or a seed is a compile error (type test); that the lines are one player's own stream is the caller's duty (the bridge hands over a `PlayerChannel`'s lines, which `playerChannel` has checked), beside the caller-supplied `WorldFactsData` (`stall_moves`, the ids of the moves that add the engine's `stall` volatile; `terrains`; `swap_side_conditions`, the list Court Change swaps; `toxic_stage_max`, the Toxic stage ceiling; `action_sources`, the `[from]` texts of the move lines the engine runs as move actions of their own (Round, Instruct, Dancer); reviewed engine data, no game rule or list in the module, and the parity test reads them from the pinned `data/moves.ts`, `data/conditions.ts` and `sim/battle-actions.ts` (`test/world-facts-data.ts`, shared with the world builder tests) and fails if the pin differs; no committed record of this data exists yet, the composition root of search supplies it; only protocol wire tokens such as `tox`, `psn` and Poison Heal's `[from]` text are in the code). Per side (`own` / `opponent` as the player sees them) and position letter: `active_turns` (the engine's `activeTurns`: `|turn|` lines since the `|switch|` or `|drag|`; a `|swap|` carries it), `last_move` (the last `|move|` without a `[from] move/ability/item` tag; `[from]lockedmove` is the Pokemon's own), `protect_streak` (consecutive turns of successful stall uses; the `stall` volatile survives one turn, a `-fail`, a switch and a faint end it), `toxic_ticks` (the `[from] psn` damage lines, or Poison Heal heals, since the later of the `-status|…|tox` and the switch-in, which resets the stage as the engine does); per side `total_fainted`, `fainted_this_turn`, `fainted_last_turn` (the engine's `faintedLastTurn`) and the side conditions with `started_turn` and `layers` (`-sidestart` lines, `-swapsideconditions` swaps them); on the field the weather, terrain and pseudo-weathers with their `started_turn` (0 before `|turn|1`; a `-weather|…|[upkeep]` line starts nothing). Remaining durations are the writers' subtraction from `started_turn`, not a fact here. **`world-facts/2` (slice W part 2, #1140)** adds what the writers need and the log shows: `move_actions` per active (the engine's `activeMoveActions`, which Fake Out and First Impression read in this pin: own `|move|` lines, `[from]lockedmove`, tagged lines listed in `action_sources`, `|cant|` lines and the confusion self-hit `-damage|…|[from] confusion`; the `-activate|…|confusion` before it is printed at every attempt and counts nothing), `volatiles` per active (`started_turn` of each `-start`, forgotten at `-end`, a switch and a faint; `moved_before`: the Pokemon had run a move action that turn, which Taunt's own start handler reads through the queue), `source` on weather, terrain, field and side conditions (the `[of]` Pokemon of the start line, else the actor of the latest `|move|` line, any tag) and `after_residual` on every dated condition and volatile (it began after the `|upkeep` of its turn, or before turn 1, so the engine has not counted it down yet: a weather set by a replacement after a faint is still full when the next turn begins), and `move_uses` per side and nickname (own move choices by move id). A volatile a Baton Pass handed over has no `-start` since the switch-in, so it has no fact (the world builder then refuses it). Typed refusals (`WorldFactsError`, a code and a line index, a fixed message that quotes no line; the first one is kept and thrown by `facts()` from that line on, earlier requests keep their facts): `bad_player`, `secret_marker`, `malformed_line`, `unattributable_line` (a move, faint, swap or switch from a position nobody holds), `illusion_replace` (any `|replace|`; the ADR makes Illusion R), `stall_unresolved` (a stall move with no `-singleturn` or `-fail` of its user before the next action) and `toxic_tick_unobserved` (a badly poisoned Pokemon in play at `|upkeep` with no tick line since the residual began: Magic Guard, or Poison Heal at full health). Tests: `packages/public-state/test/world-facts.test.ts` (hand-written lines for every fact and refusal, 100% lines) and `packages/showdown-bridge/test/world-facts-parity.test.ts` (in the bridge because it needs the engine), which replays every committed battle (random-play, `unavailable/`, Illusion, Baton Pass, Fling, golden) through the pinned engine and requires, for both seats at every request, the facts equal an oracle recomputed from the OMNISCIENT log by Pokemon name (a backward scan from the cut that matches the channel prefix, sharing no state machine with the module), the turn, weather, terrain, field and side-condition layers equal the shared reader's state, the stall, Court Change and Toxic cap data read from the pinned `data/moves.ts` and `data/conditions.ts`, and the only refusal the Illusion replace (88 of 1,960 requests at this pin); a negative control (an empty stall list) makes the sweep fail. Not here: the fidelity corpus (slice T). Limits: the corpus reaches no hazard with two layers (the hand-written tests do), and `activeTurns` is the log's count, confirmed against the engine's source (`nextTurn` increments it for every Pokemon in play) rather than against a live battle.

**Targeted fidelity corpus** (ADR-026 Evidence 1, finding N-A, slice T, #1137; `packages/showdown-bridge/src/search-fidelity-corpus.ts`, `scripts/capture-search-fidelity-corpus.ts`, `fixtures/showdown/<format>/search-fidelity/`). Seeded A1 battles (`runBattle`, both seats, `random-legal` or `pending-first` per seat, the random corpus's 30-turn cut) that reach the rare states a search world must reproduce, so each world writer and admitted id can meet its exercised-turn gate. The targets are the reviewed input `targets.json` (no id is a constant in code): per target the moves and abilities a team must hold to be drawn for it (as the reducer sweep's `--only-move`), the regular expressions over the PUBLIC channel lines that count as one occurrence (a turn counts once; lines before `|turn|1` are turn 0; `consecutive` counts a turn only when the same actor, the pattern's first group, matched on the turn before, for Protect chains) and `min_turns`; `unreachable` lists what no legal team can hold and `checkUnreachable` fails when the roster can (a pin bump that adds Download must make it a target). Targets (each `min_turns` 24, since slice F admits a writer or id after 20 exercising turns): Fake Out, Protect chains, Encore, Disable, Last Respects, Counter, Mirror Coat, weather and its expiry, screens and their expiry, Trick Room and Tailwind with their expiry, Toxic (status and ticks), Intimidate; Download is unreachable at this pin. The search (`captureFidelityCorpus`, pure in the pool, targets and `seed_base`) has two phases: it plays `attempts_per_target` battles per target (the target's team on a seat that alternates, any team opposite, the four actor mixes in turn, seeds from `seed_base`) and pools those in which the target occurs, then keeps greedily the battle with the highest gain per turn played (turns added to every target still short, capped at the shortfall) until each target has its `min_turns` or `max_battles` is reached; a target left short is logged, never lowered. Teams: the committed fixtures-v1 dev split plus a generated dev split (`--dataset DIR`; the committed one holds no Last Respects or Mirror Coat team); only the teams of kept battles are committed, each file naming its team `origin` (dataset id, canonical team hash), and the README records the generate command and the public keys. A battle file is a random-corpus file plus `actors` and `origin`; `loadRandomCorpus(FIDELITY_DIR)` reads it. `summary.json` is the coverage summary, a pure function of the committed channels and targets (`fidelitySummary`: per item turns, battles and the turns per battle, plus the total battle turns, the denominators slice F counts against); the replay test (`test/search-fidelity-corpus.test.ts`, explicit 120 s budget) re-plays every battle from its seed, teams and actor seeds, recomputes the summary and requires every `min_turns`. Occurrence is counted on player 1's channel only (public lines). Not here: the fidelity comparison itself (slice F). **Extension for the oracle's yield (#1187).** Slice F's report counts EXERCISING AND EQUAL turns (F1 and F2), which a target's occurrence count only approximates (toxic: 40 occurrence turns gave 26 exercising and 17/16 equal), so the corpus was extended and each added target names the report row it feeds. `targets.json` gained, per target, the optional `items` (a held item draws a team: White Herb), `against` (the opposite team must hold a move, ability or item: White Herb needs an opposing Intimidate user for the scenario start to consume it), `attempts` (phase-1 battles for a rare state) and `report {item, min_equal}` (the report row and the number of EQUAL exercising turns it needs in F1 and in F2); `min_turns` of those targets is an occurrence floor calibrated against the report (toxic 60, Throat Chop 80, Yawn 100, Torment 55, Flash Fire 12, Focus Energy 40, White Herb 55), and `fidelity.test.ts` holds the committed report to every `report.min_equal` (24) with none blamed or missed. `captureFidelityCorpus(..., {existing})` / `--extend` keeps the committed battles (ids and bytes unchanged), counts them towards every target, plays phase 1 only for targets still short and numbers the new battles on; its seeds start at `0x7000 + 0x1000 * committed battles`, so an extension never repeats an earlier seed, and a rerun of `--extend` on the extended corpus adds nothing. The corpus is now 200 battles (77 original plus 123 added), 2,770 battle turns, 5,528 own move decisions, about 21 MB (it was 9 MB); the greedy stopped at `max_battles` 200 and overshoots the original targets (weather 625 turns, Intimidate 150), so it is not minimal: it is the first extension that met the yield, with the teams of the same generated dev split (regenerated at a later commit, its 145 original origin teams are all present). **Admissions (report of the extended corpus, F1 equal / F2 equal):** `toxic_stage` 31/28 (class W again), start trigger `whiteherb` 49/32, volatiles `encore` 41/37, `taunt` 37/42, `throatchop` 38/28, `yawn` 30/24, `torment` 45/47 (E), `flashfire` 28/24 (E), `focusenergy` 38/47 (E); `disable` still fails (blamed 4/11).

**Scenario world builder** (ADR-026 Evidence 1, W3, slice W parts 1 and 2, `packages/showdown-bridge/src/world-builder.ts` with `world-state.ts`, `world-checks.ts`, `world-redraw.ts`, `world-refusal.ts`, `world-engine.ts`, #1138 and #1140). `createWorldBuilder({engine, format_id, scenario_id, hp_rounding, writers}).build(input)` starts a scenario battle (ADR-016: `checkScenario`'s format, started at once, no Team Preview) with the four brought Pokemon a side, built from the player's own view and a FULL opponent team: `WorldInput` is `{request` (the own move request), `team` (`ActorContext.team`), `state` (the shared reader's `PublicState`), `flags` (the reader's flagged lines; any one refuses, `reader_flagged`, unless every one is a line kind of the record's `unavailable_mechanics` row), `facts` (the world facts of the player's own channel at this request, `world-facts/2`; null when that module refused: `facts_unavailable`; another turn than the state's: `facts_mismatch`), `opponent` (`{members: [{roster_index, set}]}`, its own input type: each member names the reader's roster entry it is, so slice B maps ADR-025's `OpponentWorld` onto it; the members are exactly `teamsize` of them, cover every roster entry seen in play, and may include unseen ones)}`. It takes no live battle, `BattleRecord`, omniscient log, hidden verdict, channel or seed (a type test). The own side is placed in the request's order on the player's seat (the request's `condition`, `details` gender and `stats` give HP, status, gender and a stats cross-check against the engine), the opponent side as the actives in their slots, then the bench in the reader's order; the reader empties a slot when its Pokemon faints, so a fainted member fills an empty slot. A set with no gender takes the shown one, a shown nickname replaces the set's. **E items** (part 1), through the engine's own methods and absolute (a switch-in's weather, terrain and stages are replaced): the permanent forme (`formeChange(..., true)`) and the temporary one and Mega Evolution (`runMegaEvo`), then `faint` and `faintMessages` (the engine puts a fainted Pokemon back to its set's species, so an own fainted Pokemon gets its shown forme again: its request shows it), then status (`setStatus(..., ignoreImmunities)`), HP (`sethp`; opponent HP is `assumedHp` with the world's maximum), the seven stages, own PP (`deductPP` to the request, a fainted Pokemon's too), weather, terrain, field conditions (`addPseudoWeather`) and side conditions with their layers (`addSideCondition` once per layer), each condition's source being the Pokemon the facts name (`FactsSource`, by shown nickname; one the log does not name is the side's first Pokemon and `defaulted_sources` counts it). Whether an id is a weather, terrain, status or condition is the engine's `conditions.get(id).effectType`. **Classes (part 2).** Every state item is classified by the reviewed record `config/search/world-writers.v1.json` (the `WorldWriters` contract, below): `classify` (`world-state.ts`) reads the request, the reader's state, the flags and the facts, never a hidden value, and each item present is written (W), counted (X) or refused (R). **W writers**, each fed by the facts and each equal to the live engine's value in the live test: `toxic_stage` (R in the committed record after slice F found too few equal oracle turns, W again since #1187; the writer is live-tested: `statusState.stage` = the facts' ticks; the engine resets it at a switch-in, so a benched one is never read), `bench_status` (the engine's `setStatus` refuses a Pokemon out of play: the writer sets `status` and `statusState` and runs the engine's own `Start` handler), `active_turns` (`activeTurns` and `activeMoveActions`), `protect_counter` (the engine's stall condition added once per consecutive use, its own restart logic sets the counter, one turn of it left), `last_move` (`lastMove`, `lastMoveUsed`), `choice_lock` (the choice condition added with `activeMove` set to the last move when the world's item `isChoice`; the engine's disable event then locks the other moves, V2 compares the own request), `total_fainted` and `fainted_last_turn` (`faintedThisTurn` set so the turn rollover leaves the log's value), `condition_duration` (the engine's own length, which the source's item sets through its callback, less the turns since the log started it, one fewer when it began `after_residual`; never a fresh full duration), `volatile_duration` (the same for an admitted volatile, which is added through the engine's `addVolatile` with a pending move in the queue exactly when the facts say the target had not moved, because Taunt's start handler reads the queue), `item_removed` (a consumed or knocked-off item), `start_trigger_restore` (an item or ability that a scenario-start trigger changed goes back to the set's, only for a trigger id the record admits) and `turn_number` (`battle.turn` is set one below and the engine's own `endTurn` runs: it is the T class, resetting `moveThisTurn`, `hurtThisTurn`, `usedItemThisTurn` and the like, and runs the lock, disable and trap events that make the request). **X, counted in `resampled`:** `sleep_counter` and `confusion_duration` (the engine's own draw at the world's fixed scenario seed), `opponent_pp` (full PP, counted when the facts show a move used more than 4 times) and `bench_order` (two or more unseen living members on the opponent's bench: counted per world with a hidden bench, not only on turns with a random-switch effect, because no effect list is in code; the per-turn count is the search's, with a reviewed list of those effects); an admitted volatile of class X is added through the engine and not counted separately. **R, typed and counted** as `state_unwritten` with the item and class: volatiles named by `slot_conditions`, `locked_moves` (also a last move in that row and an own request that lists fewer moves than the set holds), `truant_slow_start` (an ability in play), `substitute`, `illusion_transform` (a phantom entry, a hiding ability on an own Pokemon in play or on any living opponent member), `unavailable_mechanics`, an item or ability that was replaced or changed (`item_replaced`, `ability_changed`: Trick, Skill Swap, Trace) and every other id (`unadmitted_id`; the committed record admits `encore` and `taunt`, the ids the fidelity oracle of slice F supports). **Checks.** V1: each set passes the target format's `validateSet`, is the engine's canonical paste, and each opponent set keeps every revealed fact and Item Clause; V2 (`world-checks.ts`): after the turn rollover the world's own request (`activeRequest`, no `rqid`, no side name) equals the real one field for field, and `enumerateCandidates` gives the same list with the same pending flags; V3: the opponent as the engine renders it for the player equals the reader's state; V4: weather, terrain, field and side conditions with their layers equal the reader's and every remaining duration the writers computed is at least 1 (a condition the log still shows has not run out); V5: the request's seat is the state's and nicknames are the shown ones. **Refusals** (`WorldRefusal`) are typed and counted, carry fixed words, the `check` (V1 to V5 or null), the `state_class`, the `item` and `redraw` (whether drawing the opponent's unrevealed sets again can fix it: a revealed fact, the Item Clause, a trapping flag or candidate list nothing in the world explains, a condition that ran out under the sampled item, a hiding or turn-parity ability of a sampled set); `counts()` has every code, `checks`, `classes`, `check_class` (`V2:-`), `unwritten` by item, `resampled` and `written` by item (built worlds) and `defaulted_sources`. **Bounded redraw** (`createRedrawer(builder, max_redraws).build(draw)`): asks `draw(attempt)` for an input, builds it and draws again after a refusal with `redraw` set, at most R more times, returning the world or the last refusal with the draws used; counts `worlds`, `attempts`, `redraws`, `exhausted`, `final_refusals` and `redrawn` by check and class. It draws nothing itself (slice B supplies the seeded stream). The number of active slots is the request's `active` length everywhere (no literal): the scenario battle must have the same on both sides or the world is `start_failed`. Known limits: an effect that starts inside the residual block before `|upkeep` is dated as not yet counted down (`after_residual` false) although part of the residual may have run (rare, V4 cannot see it); `WorldFactsData` has no committed source yet (the search's composition root supplies it; tests read the pinned engine); the own request's `trapped: true` from a trapping move (Mean Look, Block) is a volatile the reader never shows, so those worlds fail V2 on the flag (typed `V2:-`, `redraw`, 20 requests of the corpus); Disable is not reproducible (its start handler also reads the active move for Cursed Body), so it is not in the live test's admitted set; Taunt, Encore, Throat Chop and Yawn equal the live engine (the record admits none, a test admits them); Dancer and Instruct lines under the facts' `action_sources` are counted from the engine's source, not a Pokemon's name; the Copycat and Mirror Move battle-level last move is not written; a Pokemon whose Illusion has not broken is refused rather than mixed (ADR-025's disguise assignment is slice B's); `hp_rounding` must invert the Champions display (`floor(100 * hp / max)`): `ceil` does, the committed `nearest` and `floor` do not for every maximum, and V3 then refuses those worlds. Tests: `test/world-builder.test.ts` builds a world at every own move request of the committed random-corpus channels for both seats with the opponent's TRUE brought team as input (1,378 requests: 847 built and passing all five checks; 473 `state_unwritten` as R, 29 `facts_unavailable` (the Illusion replace), 20 V2 on a trapping flag, 9 `reader_flagged`; each R refusal checked against an independent reading of the input), one test per write, check and refusal; `test/world-writers-live.test.ts` plays the committed seeded battles through `runBattle` with a test-only spy on the engine's `BattleStream` and compares every W item of the world with the LIVE battle at the decision (845 worlds; Toxic stage 24, benched status 62, Protect counter 35 with one counter above 3, weather duration 120, terrain 49, field 31, side conditions 144, faint counters, a gone item 373, last move; with a test record admitting Taunt, Encore, Throat Chop and Yawn: 906 worlds; with a Choice Scarf given to one Pokemon a side: 253 locks, V2 passing); `test/world-state.test.ts`, `test/world-checks.test.ts`. Cost per built world: see #1138.

**World writers record** (ADR-026 Evidence 1, B2 and N5, #1140; `packages/schema/contracts/v1/world-writers.schema.json`, generated TS and pydantic types, samples in `fixtures/contracts/v1/world-writers/`, reader `world-writers.ts`, committed as `config/search/world-writers.v1.json`; integers only, closed). `items` classifies each of the 27 state items of the closed list (`STATE_ITEM_IDS`, the schema's enum, held equal by a test) exactly once, ascending by id, with a class (`E`, `W`, `X`, `T` or `R`) the implementation offers for it (`STATE_ITEMS`: a W item can also be `R`, which refuses a world that holds it; X likewise; `per_turn_fields` is T; the refusal rows are R), a `condition` where the writer adds an engine condition (`protect_counter` `stall`, `choice_lock` `choicelock`, `confusion_duration` `confusion`) and `ids` on the refusal rows (the engine's condition, move or ability ids whose presence is the item; `unavailable_mechanics` names protocol line kinds). `admitted` lists the ids of the generic classes, ascending by kind then id: `volatile` (class `E` add only, `W` add and count the remaining turns down, `X` engine draw) and `start_trigger` (class `W`, an item or ability id whose scenario-start change is restored). Every id not listed is R, and `toxic_stage` is W since #1187 (the oracle found 31 (F1) and 28 (F2) equal Toxic turns); the fidelity oracle (slice F, below) admits ids by evidence, and the committed record lists the admitted ids of the report (`start_trigger whiteherb`, `volatile encore`, `flashfire`, `focusenergy`, `taunt`, `throatchop`, `torment`, `yawn`; #1187), edited in place in `v1`: the record is reviewed data that no run, search identity or pinned hash outside its own test has used yet, the `admitted` list is the field slice F fills, and no schema or reader rule makes a committed record immutable (a record a run HAS used would be a new file, as `damage-aware.v1.json` is). `readWorldWriters(value, engine)` adds what the profile cannot say (every item once and in order, an offered class, `condition` and `ids` exactly where the item takes them, sorted and unique ids, no id under two rows, an admitted id resolving in the pinned engine's data, the confusion condition not admitted twice); `worldWritersHash` is SHA-256 of the JCS bytes (a vector from Python is pinned), the `world_writers_hash` of the search identity (ADR-026 §5). No game id is written in code: the committed record names them and `test/world-writers.test.ts` checks every one against the pinned engine. The record's `format_id` is the format it was reviewed for (`loadWorldWriters` refuses another).

**Locked, charging and trapping states refused (#1186; found by slice F, `world-facts/3`, `WORLD_BUILDER_FORMAT` 2).** The fork oracle's 138 F1 differences from `mustrecharge` 64, `twoturnmove` 47, `partiallytrapped` 27 and `dig` 14 (and Fly, Bounce, Dive, Solar Beam, Phantom Force, Sky Attack, Meteor Beam, `trapped`, `trapper`) came from engine volatiles the shared reader never shows and the builder neither wrote nor refused. `ActiveFacts` gained `locks` (the engine condition ids of the states a Pokemon in play is in now, sorted), derived from the player's own channel by the facts module: `mustrecharge` from `-mustrecharge` until the `|cant|…|recharge`; `twoturnmove` from `-prepare` (a `[premajor]` message such as Chilly Reception's is none; this covers every charge turn and the semi-invulnerable states of Fly, Dig, Dive, Bounce and Phantom Force) until the Pokemon's next own `|move|` (a `[from]lockedmove` one included) or `|cant|`; `partiallytrapped` on the target of an `-activate|…|move: M|[of] …` where M is in the new `WorldFactsData.partial_trap_moves` (the moves whose engine definition gives `volatileStatus: 'partiallytrapped'`, read from the pinned `data/moves.ts` in `test/world-facts-data.ts` like the stall moves) until `-end … [partiallytrapped]`; `trapped` (`-activate|…|trapped`) on the target and `trapper` on the Pokemon whose move did it. A trap holds while one of its partners is in play (it ends silently when a Pokemon leaves, which the fact follows; a Pokemon may hold several), a switch or a faint drops a Pokemon's own locks, and a trap whose trapper the channel does not show is `unattributable_line`. The ids are the engine's condition ids and the module holds no move list. `classify` (`world-state.ts`) refuses a world in which any Pokemon of either side holds a lock, as R, through the record's row for the id: `locked_moves` already named `mustrecharge` and `twoturnmove`; the record gained the R item `trapping` (ids `partiallytrapped`, `trapped`, `trapper`, checked against the engine by `readWorldWriters`; the schema enum, generated TS and pydantic types and the corpus samples follow), and an id with no row is `unadmitted_id`, never ignored. `config/search/world-writers.v1.json` hash moved (a new item; re-pinned in `world-writers.test.ts`; no committed run names the record yet, `search-config.test.ts` uses placeholders). The own request's `trapped: true` is not a refusal of its own: an opponent ability (Shadow Tag, Arena Trap) also sets it and stays the V2 evidence refusal. **Probe max-damage reply** (ADR-026 section 2, finding B1, rules N-L, slice D, `packages/showdown-bridge/src/reply-model.ts`, #1141). `createReplyModel({engine, vocabulary, config, mechanics, snapshot?, data_class, probe?}).reply(world, memo?)` is the opponent's reply in a scenario world (a `ScenarioWorld` of the world builder; it takes nothing of the real game, a type test and a source scan): a new code path beside the damage-aware actor (not the actor, not its `assume` seam, no `PublicState`, reader or channel; `damage-aware@v1`'s bytes, choices and identity are unchanged, the invariance tests pass as they were), built on the shared `damageView` (the memoised probe call and its refusal classes) and the pure `damageValue` under the weights of `config/baselines/damage-aware.v1.json` (`config` is that v1 record; its hash is the search identity's `damage_aware_config_hash`). The world is cloned (`Battle.fromJSON(toJSON())`, which rebuilds every side's request from the state as written; the world's own requests are those of the start), so the world passed in is never changed; the opponent's request of the clone goes through `decodeRequest`, `enumerateCandidates` and a `CloneJudge` on the clone (`legalChoiceSet`; the world is ours, so its verdicts reveal nothing), and only non-pending candidates are valued. Attackers and defenders are the engine's own active Pokemon: the declared set (`mon.set`, exported as the canonical paste), current HP (null for full), status, the five stages and `species.isMega`; the field (weather, terrain, Trick Room, the four side conditions, the attacker's side first) is read from the world's battle. **Rules (N-L):** one `damage` query per (move, defender) and `damageValue` of each answer; a spread move (`allAdjacentFoes`, `allAdjacent`) is the sum over the player's living actives (foes only, as ADR-018 v1; the ally is not counted), a random-target move (`randomNormal`) their floored mean, a single-target move the foe at the choice's location (the engine's `getAtLoc`: 1 is the foe in active slot 0; damage-aware v1 and v2 keep the reversed numbering, v3 and the feature builders use the engine's: see "Choice location of a foe"); an `unsupported` answer scores 0 and so does a query the engine refuses to state (a `ProbeRefusal`, counted in `probe_refusals`; any other failure, and a wrong mechanics config, propagates); a slot part is eligible only if it is `pass` or a damaging move aimed at foes, so the reply never switches and never uses a status move or a move at an ally; a slot with no eligible part in any legal candidate (a Pokemon with only status moves, a locked move with no target type) is held to its first legal part in canonical order (`forced_slots`); the highest summed value wins, the first of equal values in canonical order; when it is 0 the reply is the `actorRule` choice of the world's legal set (`kind: "all_zero"`, `all_zero` counted). `reply` returns `{choice, kind, value}` (`value` in ppm); `counts()` has `replies`, `max_damage`, `all_zero`, `forced_slots`, `candidates`, `queries`, `probe_runs`, `unsupported_queries`, `probe_refusals`. **Memo:** `newWorldMemo()` is the per-world memo of probe answers by query hash (a pure function of the query, so sharing one across replies on the same world changes no answer); `reply` makes a fresh one when none is given. **Formats for the search identity:** `REPLY_MODEL_FORMAT` (1; bump when the rules or what a world's state is read as change) and `DAMAGE_VALUE_FORMAT` (1, in `damage-aware-score.ts`; bump whenever `rollsValue`, `critChancePpm` or `damageValue` return another number for the same input; `reply-model.test.ts` pins hand-worked vectors). Limits: a temporary forme a world shows and the attacker's volatiles are not stated to the probe (the world builder refuses volatiles); one reply per call (the two uniform draws and the 1/2, 1/4, 1/4 weights are slice R part 2); KO counts are not used (the share is counted from the rolls, as v1). Tests: `test/reply-model.test.ts` (hand-worked worlds with a stub probe: spread against single, the target of a single-target move, ties in canonical order, all-zero, unsupported, a refused query, a status-only slot, a random-target move, the field read from the world, no change to the world, the memo, refusals; and 40 worlds built from the committed channels with the real probe: legal, never a switch). Cost per world: see #1141.

**Search actor, `rerank-1`** (ADR-026 sections 1, 2, 4, 6 and 7, slice R part 2, #1145; `packages/showdown-bridge/src/search-actor.ts`, `search-turn.ts`, `search-leaf.ts`, `world-facts-data.ts`, `packages/team-dataset/test/search-actor*.ts`, `scripts/search-budget.ts`). `searchActor({setup, client, battle, player, team, manifest, channel_class, seed, game, policy_id, identity_hash, sampling, play?, bank_ms?, reply_model?, on_decision?})` is an A1 `Actor` for ONE seat of ONE game (it wraps a `policyActor` for every decision it does not search). `searchSetup({engine, format_id, vocabulary, port, facts_data, data_class, ...})` joins the reviewed records (the committed `rerank-1.v1`, damage-aware v1 weights, the mechanics config, the world writers) with the opponent model's `WorldSamplePort` and checks they agree (format, the config's belief identity and prior arm, the prior's format; `SearchError` `setup_mismatch`); `searchPolicy(setup, manifest, pair, mode, play)` gives the search identity (`damage_value_format`, `world_builder_format`, `world_facts_format`, `reply_model_format`, `leaf_format` and the hashes of the other inputs, all read from the setup) and the policy id (ADR-010 preimage + `search`). **Algorithm.** A move request with at least two non-pending candidates is searched; Team Preview, a forced switch, fewer than two non-pending candidates, a belief refusal, fewer than W_min valid worlds and the A2 clock skip play the pair's own choice in its underlying mode (`sampling`/`play`, the control's mode; the policy id and `u` stream are the search policy's) and are counted by reason (`FALLBACK_REASONS`). The worker's `evaluate` on the real observation gives the prior; the top `top_k` non-pending candidates by prior (a stable sort on the canonical order: ties by canonical rank) are searched. The worlds come from `createWorldSampler` (slice B) on the harness stream `searchStream({seed, game, player, policy_id, request_seq})`. In each valid world the M replies are the probe max-damage reply (weight `max_damage_ppm`, skipped when 0) and `uniform_draws` uniform draws over the world's non-pending legal replies (`legalReplies`, added to `reply-model.ts` beside `foeRequest`; draw j of world i is `floor(u * n / 2^64)` of the first 8 bytes of SHA-256 over the JCS of `{draw, purpose: "search-reply/1", search_stream, world}`, an addition to the ADR's `{search_stream, world, purpose}` preimage so every draw has its own hash; weights `(10^6 - max_damage_ppm) / uniform_draws`, exact by the config reader). The engine seed of a world is `engineSeed(stream, world)` (`sodium,` + the first 32 hex of SHA-256 over the JCS of `{purpose: "search-engine/1", search_stream, world}`) and is the same for every candidate and reply. One turn is played on `Battle.fromJSON` of the world's serialisation (made once per world; the world is never changed) with `PRNG` replaced by that seed: `playChoices` (`search-turn.ts`) restarts the clone with a capturing `send`, chooses for the seat then the opponent, calls `sendUpdates` as the engine's stream does, and returns this seat's view of the output (`playerView`: the engine's own `|split|` rule, the secret half of its own lines and the shared half of the opponent's, so the opponent is seen only as a share of 100) and its next `|request|`. A `wait` request (the opponent must switch) is played on with the opponent's first non-pending legal choice in canonical order (`foeSwitchChoice`, the `actor-rule` choice: a forced switch offers switches only, which the max-damage reply never plays), step by step until the seat has a request or the game ends. The leaf is the game score (`outcomeScore`: win 1,000,000, tie 500,000, loss 0 ppm) when the game ends, else the config's leaf: `value-head` appends the player-view lines to the REAL channel (`context.channel`), builds the observation (`observe`, the hypothetical channel ends at the world's next own request) and asks `client.evaluate` (batch 1, one query at a time; `value_ppm` unclamped), memoised by the appended lines within the decision (`leaf_memo_hits`); `hp-ko-differential` (`hpKoDifferential`) is `500000 + floor((hp share difference + standing share difference) / 4)` in ppm over the world's brought Pokemon (integers). Score of a candidate = the sum over the scored worlds and replies of weight times leaf (the same divisor for every candidate, so the sums order exactly as the means do); the best sum wins, ties by prior rank. A world whose reply model fails (`ReplyModelError`) or whose turn the engine refuses is dropped and counted (`worlds_dropped`: `reply_model`, `choice_refused`; any other error is a defect and propagates); fewer than `min_valid_worlds` scored worlds play the pair (`below_min_valid`). **Pairs that read candidate rows (#1287):** the leaves are value-only queries exactly when the worker states `value_query` (next to the Evaluate sampling paragraph of [policy-worker.md](policy-worker.md)), so a `probe-damage/1` pair is searched: the root `evaluate` keeps its candidates and its rows, built with the new `SearchActorOptions.features` (the same `ActorFeatures` a `policyActor` seat takes, whose memo the inner pair shares), and a build failure forfeits as `crash` like the pair's own seat; `searchPolicy(..., features?)` puts the feature set hash into the policy id as `policyId` does. Still `SearchError` `features_pair` at construction: a pair that reads the opponent sheet, the belief feature set `probe-damage-belief/1`, a pair that reads candidate features whose worker does not state `value_query` (`probe-damage/2` and `/3`), and a feature pair given no `features`. **A2.** `bank_ms()` below `config.a2_clock_floor_ms` skips the search (`clock_floor`; `searchSkipReason(bank_ms, floor_ms)` is the one rule); `A2BotOptions.search = {a2_clock_floor_ms}` makes the session record `search_skipped: "clock_floor"` on a battle `DecisionRecord` whose timer snapshot shows less bank than the floor (absent otherwise and for Team Preview; an optional field, a minor change; the session still answers with the actor rule, since no policy is wired into A2, and A2 results are never evaluation). **Counts per game** (`report().counts`, integers; wall time is printed, never stored): decisions, searched decisions, fallbacks by reason, worlds asked/built/refused, redraws and ADR-025's `legality_redraws` apart, refusals by code and by `check:class`, belief refusals by the model's code, worlds dropped by reason, resampled X counters, worlds scored, turns, wait continuations, leaf queries / memo hits / game-end / hp-ko leaves, and the replies drawn; `report().reply_model` has the probe counts. **Privacy.** The decision's only inputs are `SearchInput` = `{world: WorldRequest, channel (the player's own lines), top, stream}`: the live battle, `BattleRecord`, `OmniscientLog`, hidden verdicts, the seed and the opponent's team have no field (`test/search-actor.test.ts`, a type test pinning the key sets); the seed reaches only `searchStream` (so another battle seed draws other worlds, as it draws another `u`). Nothing searched is stored: the actor returns a choice string; `test/search-actor.test.ts` of `team-dataset` shows the Episode and the training examples of a search game equal those of a replay of its choices and carry no search key. **Tests** (real engine, real opponent model, a stand-in worker whose value is the hit-point difference of the observation's own log): determinism (same choices, log and counts), invariance (opponents with the same species and other hidden sets give equal choices AND equal scores at every request with an equal player channel) with the negative control (a port whose worlds are the opponent's real sets gives different scores at every searched request and different choices at some), the fallbacks, the drops, the leaves, the second seat, the reply weights, a finished game (game-end leaves), and `search-actor.python.test.ts` (the real worker, tiny seeded networks, skipped without the Python environment). The `reply_model`, `on_decision` and `bank_ms` options are test and tooling seams. **Cost** (`scripts/search-budget.ts`): figures on #1145. In the same sample 19 of the 43 non-Preview, non-forced decisions had fewer than W_min valid worlds (all 155 refused worlds are the builder's `state_unwritten`), so the pair played them. The gate is S0's. `world-facts-data.ts` is the one reader of the world facts' engine data (the stall moves, the Court Change list, the Toxic ceiling, the action sources; the tests' `readWorldFactsData` delegates to it). Not here: progress and head-to-head integration (slice P), S0 and E-S1, search in training, Team Preview or forced switches, a pair with candidate features, the Illusion disguise assignment in a world (slice B's known limit), the trapping-flag conditioning (#1127).
