# Differential harness and backend admission

The engine-agnostic differential harness of ADR-030 (groundwork G1): the backend port, byte comparison against the pinned engine as the oracle, the `BackendAdmission` ledger, the fresh-corpus rule, the shadow sampler and the self-differential report.

Part of the architecture documentation split out of `CLAUDE.md` (index: [README](README.md)). Ticket references such as `#1461` point to Redmine (`redmine.piglor.com/projects/areneon`), where measured figures and verdicts are recorded.

**What it is for** (ADR-030 common rules 2, 4, 6 and 7; ADR-028 §5 to §7; #1461). Any non-Showdown backend of ADR-030 (step 3's damage kernel, #1457; later the step-5 battle step, via G2) may answer for training games only what it was admitted on by byte-equality with the pinned engine. This harness is the one machinery that compares, admits, revokes and shadow-checks, written once. Showdown is always the oracle. Nothing here is wired into the loop yet: `generation.backends`, routing in the batch seam and the taint record are #1457's (level 1) and the build ADR's (level 2).

**Packages and the seam.** The engine-free core is the workspace package `packages/differential` (`@areneon/differential`, depends on `@areneon/schema` only; imports no engine, bridge or network module and no Node built-in but `node:crypto`; holds no float: a source scan in `test/packaging.test.ts`, as `opponent-model`'s; named by no image or deployment file; depended on by `packages/team-dataset` alone and imported only by its `differential*.ts` files, so no evaluation path reaches it). The bridge stays the engine's owner and does NOT import the core: `packages/showdown-bridge/src/differential-oracle.ts` declares its shapes structurally (`OracleBackend`, `QueryFacts`), and the composition root `packages/team-dataset/src/differential-mechanics.ts` joins the two, as `createSearchWorlds` joins the opponent model.

**Backend port** (`backend.ts`). `Backend<In, Out>` = `{id, backend_format, backend_commit, run(batch) -> Settled<Out>[]}`: a batch in, outcomes in input order, a refusal kept as the input's outcome (the `BatchRunner` of the batch seam, `probe-batch.ts`, and `FeaturePool.run` already return exactly this). A `Level<In, Out>` says how one level is compared: `entities(in)` and `exercised(in, oracleOutcome)` (sorted `<kind>:<id>` strings; exercised read from the ORACLE's outcome), `answers(in, out)` (an output names its input), `bytes(out)` (compared by JCS bytes) and `errorKey(error)` (a refusal's class and fixed words). Level 1 is the mechanics-query level (`In` a parsed mechanics query, `Out` its `MechanicsResult`); level 2 (a battle step and its player view) is G2's adapter on the same core.

**Comparison** (`compare.ts`, `harness.ts`, `tally.ts`). `compareBatch` runs the inputs through both backends and compares item by item: `value` (JCS bytes differ; `field` is the JSON path of the first difference in sorted key order, e.g. `$.answer.no_crit[3]`), `refusal` (both refused, differently), `settled` (one answered, one refused), `answer` (the backend's output names another input: a reordered or misattributed answer), `missing` (no outcome: a dropped result, also counted as a batch `count_mismatch`). The oracle must answer every input (a `HarnessError` otherwise). `runCorpus` compares a corpus in batches into a `CorpusTally`: per entity `present`, `exercised`, `equal` (equal exercising items), `differing` and the first difference's position; per pair (fresh corpora only) `co_present`, `co_exercised`, `co_equal`; every difference with its reason, field and entities.

**Level-1 entity and exercising rule** (`mechanics-level.ts`, reviewed; the facts come from the bridge, which reads each query's own sets with the pinned engine's `importPaste` and its declared state, so no list of game content is written in the core). Entities: the query kind; each Pokemon's species forme (an evolved Mega by the forme its stone's engine data makes), ability, item, set moves, declared status and declared history volatiles; the moves the actions use; declared weather, terrain, Trick Room, side conditions and pending slot conditions. Exercising: always the kind; nothing else on a refusal or a `refused` result; the used moves on `unsupported`; a computed `stats` answer the species; `damage` the used move, both Pokemon's species, ability, item and status, and the declared field and sides; `speed_order` the same over every combatant; `move_outcome` that plus the declared volatiles and slot conditions. Unused set moves and volatiles outside a move outcome are present but never exercised (no evidence claimed for what the answer may not have read).

**The ledger** (`BackendAdmission`, `packages/schema/contracts/v1/backend-admission.schema.json`, generated TS and pydantic types, corpus samples `fixtures/contracts/v1/backend-admission/`, readers `admission.ts` `readBackendAdmission` and `areneon_contracts.backend_admission.read_backend_admission`; integers only, closed, `data_class` synthetic, `rule` `admission/1`). It names the level, the backend (`kind`, `backend_format`, `backend_commit`), `showdown_commit`, every corpus (`id`, `kind` fresh / regression / shadow, `seed`, `items`), every entity with its per-corpus counts and `admitted` flag, the listed pairs and the revocations. `admission_hash` (`admissionHash`, `admission_hash`) is SHA-256 of its JCS bytes: the hash `generation.backends` will name. **Rules** (`buildLedger` writes them, both readers recompute them): an entity is admitted iff the fresh corpus holds at least `N_EXERCISED` = 20 exercising items, all equal, no corpus has a difference naming it and it is not revoked; regression and shadow counts never admit, and a ledger that admits from them is refused with "admission evidence must come from the fresh corpus"; a pair of admitted entities present together in at least 1 in `PAIR_ODDS` = 10^4 fresh items is listed and admitted iff at least `N_PAIR` = 5 fresh items exercise both, all equal (a listed pair that is not admitted routes to the oracle; rarer pairs are covered only by the shadow check, ADR-028 §5's accepted residual); any difference revokes every entity of its item, recorded at the first corpus (in id order) that found one; `withCorpus` adds a regression or shadow tally later (admission only falls; a fresh corpus is never added later). **Pin:** a ledger whose `showdown_commit` is not the reading checkout's is refused ("every query routes to the engine"), so a pin bump empties admission. **Routing:** `admissionRouter(ledger | null).route(entities)` is null when the backend may answer, else the first unadmitted entity or blocked pair (for run metrics).

**Fresh corpus by construction** (ADR-030 rule 7, PokaiEngine's warning). `freshSeed(backend)` is SHA-256 over the JCS of `{backend_commit, backend_format, purpose: "differential-fresh/1"}`; `freshCorpus(backend, draw)` calls `draw` with that seed and nothing else and names the corpus `fresh-<first 16 hex>`. The readers refuse a fresh corpus whose seed is not the backend version's and a second fresh corpus. So the admission corpus cannot exist before the backend version is frozen, and corpora used to debug it (kind `regression`) are counted and reported, never evidence.

**Shadow sampler** (`shadow.ts`). #1195's bottom-k (`inference-divergence.ts`): an item's key is the first 8 bytes of SHA-256 over the JCS of `{key: <stable key>, purpose: "differential-shadow/1"}`, the sample the smallest keys (ties by position), so the same items are sampled whatever order they were answered in. One function; the size rule is a parameter: `LEVEL_1_RULE` max(1% of backend answers, 256), `LEVEL_2_RULE` max(5% of games, 64), never above n, integers only (`shadowSize` equals the bridge's `sampleTarget` for level 1; a test holds them equal). `shadowCheck` recomputes the sample with the oracle and throws a typed `ShadowDivergence` (its `differences` and the sorted `entities` to revoke) on any difference or count mismatch: stop the generation. Wiring it into the loop and the taint record is not here.

**Oracle and composition root** (`differential-oracle.ts`, `differential-mechanics.ts`). `probeOracle(engine, {config, snapshot?, damage_path?, id?})` is the pinned probe through `batchOf` (format `MECHANICS_ADAPTER_FORMAT`, commit the engine's); a second `loadEngine()` object has its own per-engine probe tables, so two oracles are two instances. `queryFacts(engine)` reads a query's facts. The composition root collects the queries the feature builders themselves issue: every request (but `wait`) of both seats of a committed battle is built with `probe-damage/2` and `probe-outcome/1` through their generator form (`buildFeaturesG` driven by `driveSync`, one memo per game and set, as in play), every yielded query recorded once; the oracle answers each as it is asked (`remembering` keeps its outcomes by query hash, so the builders' run and the comparison share one probe call). Every probe answer is made under the outcome set's mechanics config (a superset of `/2`'s query kinds).

**Self-differential report** (gate 1; `scripts/differential-report.ts`, `fixtures/showdown/<format>/differential/self-report.{json,md}`, generated like ADR-026's fidelity report; do not edit). The probe on one engine object against the probe on a second, over the random, search-fidelity and unavailable corpora as regression corpora; per corpus items, differences by reason and first differing field, count mismatches; per entity present, exercised, equal and differing summed over the corpora; entity kinds. Its `inputs` (harness format, pin, the feature sets, the mechanics, damage-aware and outcome config hashes, each corpus's files hash) are recomputed by `test/differential-mechanics.test.ts`, which fails on a stale report and re-renders the Markdown from the JSON; CI also runs the self-differential on the first battle of each corpus (zero differences, every entity kind tallied). The full run is about half an hour of probe work on one thread (`--part` per corpus in separate processes, then `--merge`). **Result at pin `7244fe8e`:** see the committed report and #1461 (zero differences).

**Negative controls** (gate 2, `test/differential-mechanics.test.ts`, real probe on the first random battle's queries): a backend that perturbs one roll of one move (`value` at `$.answer.no_crit[3]`, only items with that move, each naming it), one that swaps the crit vectors (`value` at `$.answer.crit[i]`), one that returns `unsupported` with another reason (`value` at `$.reason`), one that answers out of order (`answer` on every item, with the item's own entities), one that drops a result (`answer` from the dropped item on, `missing` last, a count mismatch); the shadow check throws `ShadowDivergence` on a broken backend and passes the probe. A ledger from a fresh corpus drawn by the backend's own seed over those queries admits `kind:damage`, routes, and is refused at another pin. The arithmetic edge cases (gate 3) are `packages/differential/test/admission.test.ts`: 20 against 19 exercising items, equal counts, presence without exercise, a pair at 5 and at 4 and a rare pair, revocation and its first corpus, `withCorpus` from a shadow sample, regression evidence refused, another pin refused, every reader rule; the corpus samples are read by both readers (`packages/differential/test/samples.test.ts`, `tests/contracts/test_backend_admission.py`) and classified alike by `make contracts`.

**Not here:** the damage kernel and `generation.backends` (#1457), the battle level (G2), any routing in the batch seam, any run that uses a backend.
